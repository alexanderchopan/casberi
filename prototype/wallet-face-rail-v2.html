What I think

  • The persistence half is right, and it's the whole prize. A wallet scope that survives a room switch and reaches every wallet room fixes a real gap — today no wallet room but the balance room can be narrowed at all. It also means the scope stops being a property of one room and becomes what it actually is: a filter over everything wallet-shaped.
  • The position half costs more than it pays. Above the chips, the rail can only appear in wallet rooms — a wallet scope is meaningless over a Bluesky post — so the strip changes vertical position every time you enter or leave a wallet room. The strip is the app's primary navigation and its position is half of what makes an icon-only chip findable (the 2026-07-30 ruling). Below, the same appear/disappear moves only the room's own content.
  • You still get the hierarchy you're after, because the rail is only ever there in wallet rooms. When it's visible, the reading is room → whose, and the room is already named by a lit chip. What sells "whose" is the face being lit and the card naming it — not which of two adjacent bars sits higher.
  • Either way, one thing has to change beyond position: the scope has to be readable when it isn't "All" — hence the card's kicker saying vitalik.eth · Peer in 2 and 4. Without it a scoped Peer room and an unscoped one with one busy wallet look identical.
