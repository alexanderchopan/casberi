Wallet switcher — faces instead of pills

The three-layer problem isn't that there are three bars — it's that all three are the same grammar (a glass capsule holding word pills), so they read as one confused control. Drawing the wallets as faces gives each layer its own shape: categories are words in circles, seats are word pills, wallets are faces. Borrowed from WalletScreen.rosterSlot, which already does exactly this at 60pt on the setup page.

What's encoded, and what you'd be deciding

  • Selection is opacity and weight, never a ring. §351 made selection a fill in the seat switcher and a ring in the strip precisely so the two never compete, and a dashed orange ring already means "needs reconnecting". So a selected face is full opacity with a semibold name; the rest sit at 50%. No new cue enters the vocabulary.
  • "All" wears the wallets themselves. It has no identity of its own, so it draws as the actual faces overlapped — literally what it is, no new metaphor, and it finally gives the combined balance a mark instead of a neutral grey capsule.
  • Faces go on the glass, not in it. The design law already says a mark someone recognizes stays opaque — frosting it only muddies it. Which is also why the rail needs no glass capsule of its own: faces are content, and content doesn't wear the material.
  • B versus C is the real decision, and it's about height. B is truer to the setup page you cited and never makes you look elsewhere to know whose room you're in. C costs almost nothing, and it removes a name the card underneath is already printing. B's captions can't be shown only on the selected wallet — the label slot has to stay reserved or the bar's height jumps as you tap.
