The connect page is a yes/no

The ruling: the connect page in the catalog is only about connecting. No markets, no book, no live odds — that's what the room is for. Two questions, and both appear on both pages: do you want to follow Kalshi's prediction markets, and do you want to follow Polymarket's.

This replaces prediction-catalog-v1.html. The live proof line is out — of the connect page and of the product page. Everything that showed a market on a catalog surface is gone.

What that costs, stated up front: the Following and Resolved lists have to leave the connect page too — they are markets. They already exist in the room's own feed. The one thing that genuinely lived only here was Remove its things too on disconnect; column 4 shows where that goes.

The one naming collision, and how I'd settle it

“Follow” is already spent: it's the verb for following a single market, and the room's section header is literally Following. So “Follow Kalshi prediction markets” and “Follow this market” are two different acts wearing one word. I'd keep your wording — the object disambiguates, an exchange and a question don't get confused in practice, and inventing a second verb for the catalog would cost more than the collision does. Flagging it because if it ever does read wrong, this is the sentence to change, and the alternative is to make the exchange row say Show and leave Follow to markets.

Why both questions go on both pages

  • It's the only place a single-venue user learns the other exists. The disagreement between two crowds is the one thing neither exchange's own app can show, and today nothing in the catalog mentions it.
  • It makes the pair symmetric — one component, an ownVenue parameter, two screens that can't drift.
  • It replaces the transient twin offer as the discovery path. The per-market twin can stay where it belongs — in the room, after a follow — instead of being the only way anyone ever finds out.

Held

  • No market data on any catalog surface — connect page or product page. The inert preview in column 5 is authored copy, not a read.
  • No auto-follow of anything. Saying yes to an exchange follows zero markets; it adds the chip and the room.
  • A No is a disconnect, so it asks what disconnect asks. It never silently drops markets you followed.

Still out of scope, still true

  • With both connected, the disagreement cards render only in .all scope while PredictionRoomBook defaults every room to its own venue — so the comparison these questions sell is currently behind a segment tap. If you say yes to this design, that room fix stops being optional: the catalog would be promising something the room hides.
