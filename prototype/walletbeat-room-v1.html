Walletbeat — built, with real ratings

A keyless Wallet-group seat reading Walletbeat's own published ratings. Every number, sentence and verdict below is real data read live from their registry on 2026-08-20 — not illustrative.

What the real data changed The first mock showed confident coloured strips for every wallet. Against real data that was actively dishonest: most wallets are overwhelmingly unrated. Trezor is 0 of 25 attributes judged, Ledger 1 of 25, Uniswap Wallet 1 of 29; sixteen of the 32 sit under a quarter. Only six clear 80%. A bar drawn from raw counts shows the wallet nobody has examined as the cleanest one on screen. So coverage now leads everywhere, and below a measured floor a row says what's missing in words instead of drawing a shape.
The room
The head answers "is there anything about the wallets I use I should know today, and how much has actually been checked?" — never "which is best". Its ranking is a ranking of attention: an unexamined wallet sorts low because there's nothing to read, never because it's safe. Rabby's and MetaMask's sub-lines are Walletbeat's own sentences; Ledger's says what's missing. Incidents run below, day-grouped.

The connect screen
One mode chip, one intro sentence — §315's whole prose budget. The search is instant and offline: it runs over a directory bundled at ship time, so naming your wallets needs no network at all. Only the full report card and the incidents are read live. Each result says how much has been examined, which is the fact that decides whether watching it will tell you anything.
