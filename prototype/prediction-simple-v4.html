Three ways to make it stupid simple

All three cut the same chrome: one control row (order folded in as chips beside categories, search as an icon), no freshness line, and one card shape — a race no longer renders differently from a yes/no market, it just shows its leader and you tap for the field.

They differ in how much of the book they show you at once. A is a tidier version of what exists. B stops using cards at all. C stops showing you the book by default.

A — Trim
Same shape, less of it
Cards stay, but every card is identical: question, leading answer, one big number, one bar. A twelve-way race and a yes/no market are now the same object. Disagreement is a property, not a section — a question priced on both venues just shows two bars, wherever it happens to sit in the list. Eleven elements down to five.

B — The list
No cards at all
The book is a list, in the app's own native grammar. Each market is one row, and the row's fill IS the probability — no separate bar element, no card, no padding stack. Nine markets fit where three cards did. Colour tells you the venue without a badge. Everything else lives one tap away in the preview.

C — The briefing
Don't show the book by default
The most radical, and the most Casberi: the room opens with what's worth knowing, not a browsable index. Three facts the app can only produce because it reads both books and your corpus — biggest move, a real disagreement, something closing today. The full 2,400-market book is one tap away, not the landing state.

No controls at all on the landing screen.
