The category name holds column one and nothing else ever does. Chips run in columns two through five, and a category that needs a second row picks up at column two again — so every chip in the tray shares one left edge and the names form a rail down the side. All is gone from the grid, which is what frees column one to mean one thing.

Three categories pulled out at true size. One grid, five equal columns of 65.2pt at a 402pt width; the marker owns column one on a category's first row and the column simply stays empty on a continuation row. Nothing is hand-positioned — the alignment falls out of the grid, so it holds at every Dynamic Type size and on the iPad's wider tray without a second layout.

The marker sits on the icon's centreline rather than the cell's, so the names line up with the chips beside them and not with the labels below them. Long category names have room: "Markets" and "Reading" are the widest in the catalog and clear 65pt at 13pt semibold.

What removing All costs, and the cheapest ways to pay it

The tray was the only place All carried a name. It's still the first chip in the strip behind the tray, so the room is one dismiss and one tap away — but from inside the tray there's now no way back to everything, and that's a real loss for the one entry that isn't a source.

Three ways out, in order of how little they disturb the alignment you just bought: the title does it — "Your sources" becomes a tappable row that clears the filter, no cell spent; the marker column does it — a first row whose column one reads "Everything" and whose chips are the four most recent, which keeps the rail intact but makes one marker behave unlike the rest; or nothing does it, and the strip stays the only door to All, which is honest and costs a dismiss.

Icons are simplified stand-ins; a build uses the same BridgeIcon marks, and the rings read as they do on the strip — solid tint where you are, dashed orange for a connection that needs you.
