SourcesTray.swift · structure pass, three treatments

Same twenty sources, three ways to stop it reading as a wall of icons

The shipped tray already groups by catalog category, but its gray 11pt overline is quieter than twenty colored marks — so the grid still reads first and the structure second. Three answers, in rising order of commitment: ink the titles in the tint (change nothing else), put each category on a card that wraps through the screen edge (your full-bleed idea), and give each category its own card that never splits, packed two-up.

One line of change: the overline goes from tertiary gray to the tint. A start is semibold tint, a continuation repeat (column one) is the same tint at half strength with its interpunct. The blue now competes with the icons for the first glance, which is the entire point — structure gets a color and color is what the icon wall was winning with.

The risk is what blue means elsewhere. In this app the tint is the interactive family — the active chip's ring two inches away is the same hue. A blue word above a grid of buttons can read as a button itself. If that bothers in practice, the half-step is tint for starts and keeping gray repeats.

Containment instead of typography. Each category is a soft fill behind its own chips. The rule you proposed carries the wrap: a rounded end is a real end, a cut end runs through the screen edge — Social starts rounded in row one, bleeds off the right, re-enters row two from the left, and ends rounded after X. Markets and Life do the same. The name lives inside the card, only at its start; the continuation needs no label because the card itself says "still going".

Costs to weigh: nothing else in the app bleeds through an edge, so this is a new pattern to teach; the seams between neighbours in a row are 3pt, thin enough to miss at a glance; and it takes DSTray's side padding away from this one tray. In exchange the grouping is unmissable without a single word of extra ink — and packing, order and row count all stay exactly as shipped.

Real shelves, like the catalog these were connected from. Every category is its own card, whole, never straddling anything — the strongest possible "this is a place, not a pile" read, and it echoes AppsScreen's shelf language so the tray and the catalog finally rhyme. Two columns is what makes it affordable at all: the never-split rule in a single column measured 816pt back in the v1 mock.

And it still doesn't quite pay for itself. Chips drop to 46pt to fit three across a half-width card, catalog order bends (columns balance by height, so Markets sits beside Media), and at twenty sources it lands at ~680pt — past the 620 resting cap, so the tray scrolls, which is the tax the packed grid was built to avoid. This is the prettiest option and the most expensive one.

It also breaks a standing rule, visibly. At a 48pt cell a long name can't wrap to two lines and fit — "GeckoTerminal" clips above, which is exactly the truncation prd §201 forbids on tiles. Fixable only by widening the cell (fewer per row, more height) or shortening the names, so treat it as part of C's price rather than a drawing slip.

How to choose

The problem is contrast, not information. The shipped tray already says everything these three say; it says it in 11pt tertiary gray against twenty saturated marks, and gray loses. A is the smallest fix to that — give structure a color. B fixes it with shape instead — give structure a surface — and costs nothing in height or order. C fixes it with true containment and pays in height, chip size and order.

My read: B is the strongest, with A as the no-risk fallback. A card is legible at any glance length where a colored word still has to be read; B keeps all four rows, the 5-across rhythm, strict catalog order and the no-scroll guarantee, which are the four things every earlier round fought for. Its real question is whether the edge-bleed reads as intentional on first sight — that's what this page is for judging. If it doesn't, A ships in an afternoon. C is worth keeping in the drawer for the iPad, where width is free and the masonry could go three-up without scrolling.

Icons are simplified stand-ins; a build uses the same BridgeIcon marks. Rings read as on the strip — solid tint is the active source, dashed orange (Farcaster) is a connection that needs you. "All" stays in the strip, not the grid, per the 2026-08-06 ruling. Card fills are a translucent white over the sheet — no hairlines anywhere, per §8.
