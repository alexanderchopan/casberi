Notifications v2

Three classes, and nothing else ever fires: an alarm (something needs you), an arrival (money or attention landed for you), and one daily whisper. All local — scheduled by the app's own background sweep, no server.

Changed from v1 — the avatar: the left-hand icon is the real Casberi berry, and it is the same on every notification. iOS puts the sending app's icon there; it cannot be swapped per source. (The one exception, communication notifications, is reserved by Apple for actual messaging apps — using it for likes risks review.)
Changed from v1 — the thumbnails: nothing is drawn by us. A notification carries an image only when the thing already holds one — a liker's real profile photo, a screenshot's own stored thumbnail. Stripe, wallets and Privacy Pools carry no image at all, because none exists. The green and grey tags under each row below name the provenance.

What fires, and what it carries

CLASS
FIRES ON
ATTACHMENT
Alarm
A dispute opened · a new approval on a watched wallet · Privacy Pools cleared or asked for proof · a deadline inside 72h (evidence due, veAERO vote, lock expiry) · payments went silent
None. Not one of these sources serves an image, and drawing one would be inventing status.
Arrival
Money in — an inbound transfer to a watched wallet, a Stripe payout paid. Attention in — likes, replies and new followers on your own posts, batched per post
Money: none. Attention: the liker’s or replier’s real avatar, already hydrated by the inbound read.
Whisper
Once a day, at a time you pick. The line DayBrief already composes — nil when there is nothing to say, and then nothing fires at all
The newest screenshot’s own stored thumbnail when the day landed one; otherwise none.

Never fires: a landed row (a post, a link, a screenshot — the feed is the product and it waits), any count or balance reading (a state, not an event — §216), a trending token, a bridge finishing a sync, a charge succeeding. Batching: likes on one post collapse into one notification that updates in place as more arrive; a sweep that finds eleven alarm-class events sends the worst one and says “and 10 more”. Time-sensitive is claimed by the deadline alarms alone, so a 3am dispute can break through — everything else honors quiet hours. The ask comes the first time an alarm-class event actually exists, never at launch.
