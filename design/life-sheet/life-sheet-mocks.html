Life thing sheets: what they are today, and three anatomies to replace them

Life is 14 seats across 7 groups — Photos, Calendar, Reminders, Gmail, iCloud Mail, Apple Health, Strava, Cal.com, Calendly, Todoist, Files, Dropbox, Contacts, HomeKit — and they present through seven content anatomies, two of which do not exist at all.

The pattern underneath is one thing, and it explains the "database field" feeling exactly: every Life bridge joins its facts into a display string at ingest and throws the parts away. "14:00 · Studio", "Designer · Studio · a@b.com", "Lock · Front door · Reachable". The sheet can only reprint what ingest already decided — so it prints it, in an 80pt label column, twice.

Content anatomy is ThingContent.swift's kindSwitch; the spec table above it is ThingSheetView.specTable.

F1An event's clock is a string, not a date.

ScheduleIngest.eventLine formats startDate at ingest; dueAt is left nil by documented convention (an event carries its start as capturedAt). The end date is never stored anywhere. So the sheet cannot say "Thursday", cannot say "in 2 hours", and cannot draw a duration — it can only reprint "14:00 · Studio". Which it then does twice: once in ScheduleCard, once as the spec table's "When" row.

F2The join link is stored and no screen reads it.

enrich(_:with: EKEvent) writes event.url to externalLink and its own doc says so: "nothing DRAWS this field today". The one reader is episodeVerb, scoped by source to Podcasts and RSS. The single most useful thing about a meeting sheet, ninety seconds before the meeting, is already in the record.

F3Contacts and HomeKit have no anatomy at all.

Neither .contact nor .accessory has a case in kindSwitch. Both land in default:, which renders content as grey prose. HomeKit's row is live state the bridge re-reads every foreground — and it renders identically whether the lock is reachable or not.

2Three anatomies

Life splits three ways by nature, not by kind: a moment with a clock (events, bookings, workouts, reminders), something that persists (contacts, accessories), something that arrived (mail, files). One anatomy each covers all thirteen remaining seats.
A · The stub — a moment with a clock
Ticket anatomy. The date block and the time range lead, with the relative distance said out loud. Below the perforation, the facts as rows with their own glyphs — place, calendar, guests — then the join link as a real control. Replaces the "When" spec row entirely.

3The fourth anatomy — the frame, for the things made of pixels

Three Life sources land pictures: Photos (screenshots), and Files / Dropbox (a folder of images — the room's own treemap reads their OCR). Photos already has most of this anatomy, which is why it was called out of scope above — but grounding it turned up a defect, and the placeholder state has never been designed at all.

F4A Files image draws its picture and nothing can open it.

The framed, tappable treatment is gated on thing.kind == .screenshot (ThingSheetView.framedShot), and so is the Zoom verb. FilePictureContent is a bare Image — no frame, no tap, no disc. So a screenshot and a folder-picked PNG are the same pixels in the same field and behave differently: one opens full screen, one is a dead picture you can only stare at. This is §275's bug wearing new clothes — the "Open in Photos" disc that did nothing, invisibly — except here the picture is already in hand, so there is nothing to gate on.

Photos · a screenshot (the frame, today + the fact)
Closest to right in the whole category: the picture is the identity, the title is the caption, the frame floats in the source's wash, and a tap opens it full screen. The addition is ScreenshotFacts — the date the OCR text names, as a row you can hand to Calendar.

F5"In your photos" is a claim, and sometimes a false one.

The placeholder fires for four different reasons — access denied, access limited and this shot not shared, the original deleted from the library, or a demo row with no asset — and says the same sentence for all of them. When the original is gone the sentence is simply wrong: it is not in your photos. The OCR text is still on the record and still searchable, which is the honest thing to lead with in that state, and the two recoverable causes are one tap from fixed.

5What this costs

Free — the facts are already in the record, nothing new is stored:
  • Event start as a real Date — it's capturedAt, which is why the relative line ("in 2 hours") and the day block need no migration.
  • Event notes (summary, already drawn), attendees (enrichedText), calendar name (tags), join link (externalLink — needs a reader, not a field).
  • Reminder due, list, priority, notes — all structured already.
  • Mail sender (authorHandle); file thumbnail (previewImageData).
Needs the string un-joined at ingest — a bridge change, still no new schema:
  • Contacts: line(for:) joins job title, org, email and phone into content. Un-joining them is what makes the rows tappable — and it is the difference between a card and a sentence.
  • HomeKit: line(for:home:) joins category, room and reachability. Reachability is the one the pill needs.
  • Health / Strava: distance and duration are computed and formatted into the title. The metric band needs them as numbers.
  • Calendar: the location is inside eventLine's ·-joined string.
Genuinely absent — one field, and the only one:
  • An event's end date is never stored. Without it the stub can show a start and no duration — "14:00" rather than "14:00 – 15:00 · 1 hr". Additive optional, no migration (SwiftData infers it), but it is a CloudKit Production deploy before it syncs.
Everything else above ships without touching the schema.
One thing in the mock that is not grounded, and should not ship as drawn. The run's pace line is decorative — no per-kilometre split, heart-rate series or route is stored on a Health row, and nothing in HealthIngest reads one. Drawing a curve there would be the §83 fake status in the one place a reader would believe it instantly. Either it comes out, or it becomes a real read (HKWorkout hands over splits in the same fetch) — a separate decision, not a rendering one. Distance, duration and pace are real and already computed at ingest; the metric band stands on its own without the curve.
And one deletion. The spec table's "When" and "Due" rows exist only because the content anatomy was too thin to carry the fact. All three anatomies above absorb them — so those rows come out, the same way "From" already stood down when the reception block started saying it better in words (prd §363).

6What shipped — and the four places it differs from this page

Built 2026-08-12 as prd §365. Everything above is in, plus the demo rows that make it visible. Four deliberate departures from these mockups, each worth keeping:
1 · The perforation is gone. The stub's dashed edge was the strongest part of the ticket metaphor and it cannot ship: build brief §8 bans hairlines with zero exceptions, and a dashed rule is a line. The halves separate the way everything else in this app separates — a slightly stronger fill on the clock band than on the facts below it.
2 · The pace curve is gone, as flagged above. Distance, duration and pace are real; the sparkline was not, and nothing in HealthIngest fetches a split series. The metric band ships without it.

Re-decided, and a correction: this page earlier said splits "come back in the same fetch". That is wrong — fetchRecentWorkouts returns HKWorkout summaries over 90 days, and a split series is a separate HKQuery per workout. At ingest that is N extra queries on a path that runs every foreground, plus a stored series, plus a CloudKit Production deploy for a field that could never be removed — three costs for the smallest of the three readings. The version worth building reads it lazily on sheet open, one query for the one workout in front of you, storing nothing.
3 · An all-day event needed a signal of its own — found while wiring the stub, invisible in every mockup here. EventKit reports an all-day event's start as midnight, so a stub reading only the dates draws "00:00" and states a time nobody meant; endAt can't rescue it either, because EventKit's end is the last instant of the final day and "00:00 – 23:59" is the same wrong answer wearing two clocks. So all-day is an explicit ThingFact.Action. The forward-compat behaviour is what makes that shape safe: a build predating the case decodes it to .none and draws an ordinary row reading "When · All day", which is still true.
4 · ContactsIngest never healed, which would have made the whole Contacts half a no-op. It skipped any ref it already had — correct while a row's entire content was one string built at first sight, wrong the moment there was anything to keep current. Left alone, the un-joined facts would have reached only contacts added from that day on, in the one corpus where almost every row is already there.
Verified: build green; CloudKit schema audit (60 properties), SwiftData liveness audit, demo self-test (74 seats, 23 feed shapes), design-motion, setup-copy, keychain, receipts-coverage, network-reach, catalog-sync and the pure-logic self-tests all pass. Not run: the simulator sweep — and verify.sh as a whole currently stops at the PRD index audit on §366/§367 citations belonging to two other sessions working in this repo at the same time, not to this change.
