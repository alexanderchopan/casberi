Casberi · the day brief · the Cash App test

The brief has the wallet's disease, and one organ the wallet doesn't

The disease. "How's my day?" composes up to seven modules — synthesis notes, money hero, the movers/next pair, the source mix, a mention card, a reading card, an hour strip — and each one arrives as its own captioned surface. Same failure the wallet room had: near-identical weight stacked seven deep, so rank is asserted by order alone and nothing leads.

The organ. The wallet room has one subject, so one number can own it. The brief's subject is the composition — the treemap, the curve, the hour shape are the answer, not decoration. Cash App has almost no charts. Applying its grammar literally would row-ify the brief into an inbox. So the three options below sit at three points on one axis: how much drawing survives the de-carding.

A · The one list — the literal transfer

Money owns the top exactly as it does in the wallet room; everything else becomes a row under a small gray label. The treemap collapses to one stacked bar with a key, the hour strip is gone, the mention and reading cards become two rows in "Worth opening", the movers tile becomes three rows that still draw their own move.

Reads fastest of the three and rhymes perfectly with the new wallet. But the day stops being a composition and becomes an inbox of nouns — and the top of the screen is now the same $ total the wallet room already crowns, so the brief looks like the wallet room wearing a date.

Cost: the hour strip (the only module that answers "when"), and the brief's own identity.

B · The sentence is the hero — the divergence

The crown goes to the day, said in words, not to the money. The synthesis notes stop being a card of glyph rows and become the one display sentence at the top; the hour strip rises to sit directly under it as a bare full-bleed band — the day's shape as the hero's own graphic, no card, no caption. Money keeps its place but as a labelled group of rows with a count-link to the wallet room, where the big number properly lives.

This is the option that lets the two rooms rhyme in grammar without competing for the same crown: the wallet room's hero is a number, the brief's hero is a sentence, and both are followed by one list.

Cost: the treemap and the source mix (money's composition is a tap away; the day's mix survives only in the sentence). Risk: the lede is model-adjacent prose in the biggest type on screen — it must stay deterministic, or the whole screen inherits the doubt.

C · Bare graphics — de-card, delete nothing

Every module survives, including all three drawings. What dies is the container, universally: no module gets a surface, a caption bar, or padding of its own. Each becomes a small gray label with its content drawn straight onto the page — the sparkline and treemap under "Your money", the source mix under "What landed", the hour bars under "When", rows everywhere else.

Rank stops being asserted by chrome and starts being carried by size and position: the money block is tall and first, the day's mix is half its height, the hour strip is a thin band, the leads are rows. Same seven modules, one column, one voice.

Cost: the least — nothing is removed. Risk: it's still long, and two treemaps on one screen invite "which map is this?" — the second must be visibly shorter and differently labelled, or they read as a repeat.

What makes it feel cumbersome — the census

SurfacesA full brief composes 7 modules, each its own captioned surface: DayNotes, MoneyHero, TilePair (2 tiles side by side), SourceMix, the mention Widget, the reading Widget, Bars. A → 0 cards. B → 0. C → 0, but 3 bare graphics.
CaptionsSix of the seven name themselves ("Watchlist", "Up next", "What landed", "Reading", "Bluesky · mentions you", "When it landed") — and several then repeat the noun inside. In a labelled list the row's own title is the caption; the gray label only says which group you're in.
Money, twiceThe hero's total, its delta and its treemap are the same read the wallet room now crowns. Today both screens open with the same $ number in the same face. B settles this by ranking money as a group inside the day; A and C keep it as the visual lead and accept the echo.
"There's more"The brief carries a chevron on a lead row, an AskMore text link, a TilePair tile that opens a screen with no chevron at all, and tappable notes with no affordance. Same 4-grammar sprawl the wallet pass cut to two: a chevron on a row, a count-link on a label.
TypeEvery row title moves to rowTitle17 (the new SF Rounded semibold from the wallet pass) so "tappable" registers before the words are read — the one rule that transfers unchanged, and the cheapest single change here.
ColorToday the brief tints notes, tiles and stat rows. Under the wallet's rule color survives only where direction is real: the wallet delta, a mover's move, the overdue mark. A flat move takes no color (§83), a count never does.
Nothing lost (C)Synthesis notes, treemap, balance curve + delta, settled-transaction row, source mix, mention post in full, reading lead + "See the rest", hour strip, keep pill — all still present. Only their containers changed.

The one thing the wallet pass can't lend. "Fewer containers" works there because the room has a single subject and a single number that can outrank everything. The brief's subject is plural by construction — it exists to say what happened across everything. So the transferable half is the grammar (one row anatomy, one chevron, rounded face = tappable, color only on real direction, rank by position under a gray label) and the non-transferable half is the crown: copying "one big number owns the screen" hands the day to the wallet, which is the one module that already has a room of its own.

Render-depth law still applies. Whatever wins, these draw at the head of the agent's Stack through GenRender's generic Widget/Row mount — the path that has now tipped the main-thread stack three times (CLAUDE.md). De-carding helps (fewer nested surfaces, fewer erasures), but any new flat renderer must be a plain HStack of Text, exactly like WalletRow.

And the whisper. The capsule that teases this screen already says the brief's sentence in one line ("Your Wednesday · 14 new, wallet +1.5%"). Option B's hero is that line, promoted — which is either the strongest argument for it (the tease and the screen finally agree) or an argument that B repeats the thing the user just tapped. Worth deciding deliberately rather than inheriting.
