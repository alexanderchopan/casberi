C · Themes own their color — identity everywhere

The Themes map stops hiding in a collapsed line: expanded by default, each cell wearing its project's own hue as a wash, sized by count — the wallet treemap's grammar with ProjectHue doing the identity work it was minted for. Uber's part is the hierarchy around it: day headers louder, counts quieter, chrome silent.

Risk: same re-litigation as wallet treatment C — colored fills were once ruled noise, but that ruling predates the magnitude wash. One reviewer should rule both rooms the same day so the two maps agree.

Moves shared by all three (independent of which frame wins)

Counts leave the sentence

"Wallet · 17 transactions" becomes Wallet with 17 trailing in rounded tabular figures — the wallet's "amounts join the stream" move, applied to every bundle row. The next event's time rides the same slot in attention amber.

The day gets a number

Day headers carry their count as a quiet figure beside the word, not inside it — "Today · 23" reads as a fact, and yesterday's 125 explains itself before you scroll.

Captions get quieter

Source names and sublines drop a step in size and ink. The gap between loud (numbers, titles) and quiet (labels) is the whole hierarchy, and today's gap is too small.

One hero, still

The existing rule survives every frame: headline, mosaic, and expanded map never stack — each frame picks its one overview, and the guard that keeps two heroes off a source feed keeps them off All.

What the law keeps in every frame: no hairlines, sentence case, glass only on the floating layer, and hue doing only its two jobs — identity (Casberi's blue for the pulse, a source's brand tile, a theme's ProjectHue) and state (the amber "next up", green and red staying reserved for money direction). Every number on every screen is a count the feed already computes; nothing is invented, ranked, or scored.
