Notes: the sheet you open to read something, that doesn't show you the thing

Wallet became a receipt, social became a post, work became a status. Notes is the category where the sheet's whole job is to show you words — and it is the one that shows the fewest of them.

Six sources land here. Every one of them renders its body in textSecondary at callout15 — the caption tier — and four of them clamp it at lineLimit(12) with no way to read the rest. The spec table underneath contributes exactly one row: From — written by you.

2Seven defects, all of them silent

Ordered by what they cost a reader. Nothing here fails a build, an audit or a screen sweep — every one of these renders perfectly.

Kindle throws the passage away data loss

title = titleLine(body) cuts at 80 characters and appends “…”, and content holds the book name instead. The rest of the highlight is stored nowhere. A long marked paragraph is not hidden — it is gone, and a re-import can't recover it because the ref dedupes on the full body's hash.

The metadata line is parsed for Added on and nothing else, so page and location are dropped too.

Four sources cut the body at 12 lines unreadable

Obsidian got an exception on 2026-08-09; Day One, Apple Journal, Kindle and shared notes never did. So the app will index a 900-word journal entry, answer questions about it, surface it in search — and then refuse to show you more than a dozen lines of it, with no “more” and no scroll.

Prose is set as a caption type

Every body here draws at callout15 in textSecondary. That is the tier the design system uses for footnotes under a fact. On these six sources the body is not an annotation of the thing — it IS the thing, and it is the quietest text on the screen.

A journal entry has no date identity

An entry's date is what it is. The sheet's only clock is the eyebrow's relative age (“3y ago”) — so the one fact that identifies a journal entry, and the only fact every one of these sources carries, is the one it never states.

Day One prints its first line twice stutter

title(from:) takes the entry's first line; content keeps the whole entry including it. The stutter guard only catches an exact whole-string match, so it fires for a one-line entry (showing nothing but a title) and misses the two-line case entirely.

Tags are stamped and never drawn invisible

Obsidian lands frontmatter and inline tags; Day One lands the export's own. The tag filter can reach them, the sheet cannot show them. Nothing on a note sheet renders thing.tags at all.

The spec table's one row the database field

On a note, every gate fails but hasFrom. The whole table is From · written by you behind an 80pt label column — the exact shape the wallet and social passes deleted, still standing here.

3The proposed shapes — three, and deliberately no fourth

Same rule the social pass used: a shape exists only where the sheet would otherwise draw the wrong noun. The gate is a data test (does this record carry prose, does it carry a date that is the record, does it quote somebody else), never a source list — so a note seat that ships next month is covered for free.

5What it costs

  • Already stored, just unread: the full Obsidian body (enrichedText), every source's tags, capturedAt (the date hero), the vault path (sourceRef), the audio bytes, and every wikilink/backlink (NoteLinks, computed at open already). Word count and read time are arithmetic over text we hold. Zero new fields.
  • “That day” is a query, not a feature: the same-calendar-day fetch the Today brief already performs, scoped to one date. It is the single strongest argument that a journal entry belongs in a corpus rather than in a journal app — and it costs one predicate.
  • One importer fix, and it is the only real work: Kindle must land the passage in content and the book where the room can rank it, and parse page/location out of the metadata line it already reads for the date. Book → authorHandle is an existing stored property, which also gives Kindle a “Whose books you mark most” room head for free. Existing rows are lossy and can't be healed in place — a re-import fixes them, and the sourceRef hash is stable across it, so nothing duplicates.
  • Deleted, not deprecated: the From row on a note, wherever the reception sentence composes. Two views drawing one fact is how a screen starts contradicting itself.
  • Mechanical, for the reason everything here is: NoteSheet.swift Foundation-only, compiled whole by scripts/note-sheet-selftest.sh in verify.sh. Every failure in this category is a silent wrong answer that renders perfectly — a passage classified as a title, a body clamped at twelve lines, a word count over a truncated string — and the simulator can see none of them.

6Deliberately not built

A shape exists only where the sheet would otherwise draw the wrong noun.

  • Day One photos and Apple Journal media. Both exports carry them and neither importer reads them. That is an import gap, not a sheet ruling — landing pixels means ImportMedia, a scoped-folder read and a size budget, which is its own pass.
  • Markdown rendering. Obsidian's body is stored as raw markdown and drawn as plain text. Rendering it is a real improvement and a genuinely separate question — it changes what the words are, not what the sheet is, and it needs its own decision about wikilinks, embeds and Dataview blocks that would silently render as garbage.
  • Apple Notes' origin. Shared notes land under source You with no record of the app they came from. Fixing that is a share-extension change, and it needs a ruling first: whether “You” means “you wrote it” or “it came in by hand”.
