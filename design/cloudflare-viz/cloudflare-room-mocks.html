The room is all alarms and no estate

Every row this bridge lands is something about to break, so a healthy account renders as an empty screen and an unhealthy one as an undifferentiated list of warnings. All three cards below fix the same thing: show what you own, not only what's wrong with it.

They share one idiom and split time three ways — A is the future (what breaks next), B is the present (what's exposed right now), C is the past (when any of it last moved). Every mark on every card is one real object: a certificate, a DNS record, a change that happened. Nothing is normalised, ranked by inference, or interpreted.

A — The runway · what breaks next

One shared 90-day axis, drawn once, with a mark where each deadline actually falls — not a bar per row (§292 ruled those out, "too AI"). The rows underneath carry the names, so the rail never needs labels of its own.

The empty state is the reason to build it: "nothing expires for four months" is a real answer, where today's room shows a blank screen that could equally mean the token is broken. The §291 empty-read ruling, in the room that needs it most.

A certificate reads differently from a registration on purpose: one is supposed to renew itself, so lateness is the fault; the other is a decision you make. The only chip is No auto-renew — the single fact that changes what you'd do.

Costs nothing. Every date is already on a landed row. The card is a second reading of what the room holds.

B — The surface map · what's exposed now

One dot per real record, never a bar: 34 dots because there are 34 records. A filled orange one is a record answering from your own server with Cloudflare stepped out of the way — which is exactly the state your own diff already treats as worth reporting when it flips.

This is the estate the room is missing. It's also the only card here that says something about a healthy account: three domains, thirty-four records, nine of them direct.

The honest ceiling belongs in the copy: MX and TXT records can never be proxied, so counting them as "exposed" would inflate the number every account on earth shows.

Costs nothing. CloudflareDNSLedger already persists [type, name, content, proxied, ttl] for every record in every zone. One small change: it stores zone ids, so the zone name has to be written alongside.

C — The change spine · when it last moved

One tick per change that really happened, on a shared 90-day axis, one strip per zone. The idiom is the address book's spine, and the reading is stability: a domain that hasn't moved in four years and one that moved this morning are two very different objects, and the room currently shows them identically.

It is dated from Cloudflare's own modified_on, not from the app's first-seen — so it's honest on the day you connect, instead of claiming a quiet 90 days on an account it met an hour ago.

The weakest of the three on a healthy account: an estate that never changes draws an empty card, and unlike A that emptiness isn't news, it's the normal state of DNS.

Costs nothing new. modified_on is already read on every DNS pass and thrown away — the ledger keeps five fields per record and that isn't one of them. Landed change rows carry the rest.

What holds across all three

  • One hue, and it's Cloudflare orange — the §292 ruling applied here. The tension worth calling: #f6821f sits next to DS.attention (#ff9f0a), so a brand mark can read as an alarm. The alternative is no hue at all and the plain text ramp, which every one of these still works in.
  • No bars, no rails down rows, no green/red. Nothing here is a gain or a loss; every card is a reading.
  • No inference. No risk score, no "your most exposed domain", no ranking of zones by how many problems they have. Each card counts things the corpus already holds and draws one mark per thing (the §295 ruling: limit the analysis, it should be factual).
  • Each says what it didn't cover. The 10-zone cap and a failed registrar read are already reported by the probe; a card that silently spoke for zones it never read would be the same lie lastCovered exists to prevent.
  • Flat by law — a plain VStack like WalletApprovalExposureCard, no generic Widget/Row mount, and no stored Thing in the view (value types only, so liveness corollary 5 has nothing to guard).

If only one ships

A. It's the card the bridge was built for — every row it lands is a date, and nothing currently draws them together. It's also the only one whose empty state is worth reading, which matters because most accounts are healthy most of the time, and a room that's blank when everything is fine is a room nobody opens twice. B is the natural second: it's free, it's the only picture of the estate, and it answers a question the alarms never do.
