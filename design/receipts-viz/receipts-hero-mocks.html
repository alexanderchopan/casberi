Three ways to lead the receipts screen

All three read only what NetworkLedger already stores — one row per host with a count, a first-seen, a last-seen and (sometimes) the service that asked. No new field, no new request, nothing added to the ledger.

That constraint is the design. The ledger deliberately keeps no per-request timestamps — its own doc calls a timestamped log "a behavioural timeline in cleartext UserDefaults", so a requests-per-hour chart is not available and should not become available. Everything below is derived from counts and two dates.

What I'd pick, and why

B. This is a header slot above rows that already carry per-service detail, so the lead's job is to state the verdict, not to re-rank what follows. B puts the one fact the screen exists for — nothing was reached that isn't disclosed — in the largest type on the page, and it's the only one of the three that says it at all. A restates the rows in a prettier form; C answers a genuinely new question but it's a different question, and it's the one that pushes against the ledger's own no-timeline rule.

If the goal is §297's "every visualization draws itself", A is the one that earns the room a face — and A and B are not exclusive: A's treemap under B's headline line is one card, about 240pt.

  • Undeclared changes the whole card, in every variation. The green pill goes orange, the number becomes the undeclared count, and the cell/segment for it is the one non-tint colour on screen. That case is why this screen exists.
  • An empty ledger draws nothing. A fresh install has no rows; the card is nil, not a zero — the same rule the room heads follow.
  • "Services", not hosts, is the unit in every headline. 26 hosts and 14 services are both true and the second is the one a person can act on; the host count stays in the subline.
