Fewer containers, same reads

The Wallet feed today stacks up to six translucent cards of near-identical weight — balance, Worth a look, Each wallet, holdings + concentration, Aave, Morpho — before a single transaction row. Each one carries its own caption, its own padding, its own chevron. When everything is a parcel, nothing leads.

Cash App's grammar is the opposite: one number owns the screen, everything else is a row. No card holds a card. The rounded money face marks anything tappable. Color appears only on a real gain and on the one live risk. Nothing below is deleted — the treemap, sparkline, delta pill, warning badges, Aave/Morpho stats and the switcher all survive. They just change container.

A · One list — the full borrow

The number is set on the page, not in a box, so the pour behind it is the hero's own background. The treemap goes full-bleed under it — the one graphic on the screen. Then everything else is the same row: 30pt round glyph, rounded-bold title, gray subtitle, trailing value or chevron.

Worth a look, Aave, Morpho and Each wallet stop being four cards and become six rows under three tiny gray labels. Reads that used to shout by having a surface now rank by position instead. "See all" folds into the Activity label as 128 total › — the section header is the door.

Costs: this reverses prd §160 ("i like both boxed"). §160's argument was that parcels beat strata when the eye is scanning for sections — true when there were six sections. With one list and three labels there is nothing to scan between.

B · Three cards, ranked — keeps §160

The boxes stay; there are just three of them instead of six, and they stop being peers. The balance card absorbs the two things that only make sense next to the number: the per-wallet split becomes two face chips (a whole card's worth of information in one line), and Worth a look becomes a strip inside the card's own bottom edge — present when it's present, gone when it isn't, never a floating third parcel.

Aave and Morpho merge into one "Lending" card as two rows. They were never two subjects; they were two providers of one subject, and giving each a full card with its own three-stat layout was the clearest case of a component picking its container.

Lower risk than A — no ruling reversed, one component (the Each-wallet card) restated as chips, one card merged. Do this if you want the win without re-opening the boxed/unboxed question.

C · The manager — shelf, two verbs, one list

The 2026-07-23 mock asked for four slabs and one sentence; then the address book merged onto the page (07-24) and it grew back to nine distinct blocks: shelf, count line, field, "Just name it" link, Connect button, peek chip, promise, Connection door, status rows, then a headed section with a sort menu, starred rows and a two-sentence footer.

Here the address book is the page's list rather than a section on it — same rows, same star, same sort, but no header card, no explainer paragraph (the footer's "tap ★ to watch" is taught by the shelf filling when you do), and no Copy button competing with the star for the trailing slot (copy moves to long-press, which the rows already carry a context menu for).

Two secondary verbs come off the page: "Just name it" becomes the field's own second action once you've typed a real address, and "Peek at vitalik.eth" becomes placeholder text in the empty field. The shelf slots now wear their wallet's value — the one fact the roster was missing.

TypeCash App's real tell isn't spacing, it's that the rounded money face marks anything tappable, not just money. Both options put every row title in ui-rounded semibold and leave the text face for gray sublines only — so "tappable" becomes something the eye feels before it reads.

Law check. No hairlines — list rows are separated by whitespace, not rules. Sentence case, no eyebrows, no kerning.
