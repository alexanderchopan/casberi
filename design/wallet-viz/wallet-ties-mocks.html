One card, the spine. It counts and it draws — no ranking, no "most of all", no reading of what any of it means. Every line on it is either a number the corpus holds or a name you gave something.

Connected means they transacted. Not "both used Uniswap", not a shared counterparty — an edge is drawn only where a real transfer between those two addresses landed. The count is addresses that reach more than one of your wallets, since every counterparty in the book reaches at least one by definition and counting those would just be the book's own length.

Amounts and counts are stated, never compared. Nothing is sorted by "importance" — the order is the book's own.

Two states it has to have — one, and none

At one connection the spine still draws and the card offers the only action it ever offers: name the address, when the connected one has no name. That's not a suggestion about what it might be — it's the book's own naming flow, reachable from the row that just became interesting.

At zero the card is one sentence — "None of your addresses are connected." — and with one watched wallet, or no landed transfers, it doesn't render at all.

What the card says, exactly

A headline count. Each connected address with its transaction count. Each of your wallets with the total that moved. One line saying since when. Nothing else — no ranking, no superlative, no inference about what a connection implies.

What "connected" is defined as

An address in the book that has landed transfers with two or more of your watched wallets. Purely the corpus: every edge is a real transaction that already exists as a thing. Shared counterparties are deliberately not an edge — "these two both dealt with someone else" is an inference, and this card doesn't make any.

Excluded: flagged transfers (a duster who hits four of your wallets from one address would otherwise land himself at the top of this card) and contracts the app can already name (Uniswap is connected to all four of your wallets and that fact is worth nothing).

Placement and cost

Last card in the Wallet screen's list, after the book rows, before the agent-bar inset. Free — no request, no new field. AddressActivity.history(for:in:) already gathers one address's transactions for the address card; this is the same walk aggregated across the book, with totals from transferUSD.
