The rail is gone

Fair — a coloured bar down the side of every row is the house style of every AI-generated dashboard, and it was doing a job the words already do. What's left is the Uber anatomy without it: heavy two-line headline, rows ranked by money at stake, one white action.

The only open question is how loudly unlimited is marked, since that's the word the rail was standing in for.

A · The chip — one yellow mark

Unlimited wears a small black-on-yellow block; capped wears a dim grey one. The yellow is the same one the kicker uses, so the card spends exactly one hue — on its own name, and on the one word that changes what you'd do about a row.

Keeps the glance. Four bright blocks and one dim reads in a half-second, at four rows or fourteen — which is what the rail was actually for.

B · The word — nothing is filled

No blocks at all. "Unlimited USDC" is bright text, "Capped at 500 USDC" is dim text, and the whole card is type on black.

Quieter and more literal — the capped row can state its actual cap inline, which the chip version has to drop for width. Costs the half-second scan: at a dozen approvals you'd be reading rather than glancing.

My call

A · the chip. The rail was redundant because the tag was already there; removing the tag as well leaves the card with no state marker at all, and "unlimited" is the one fact that changes what you do about a row. One inverted block, matched to the button, is the smallest thing that still reads at a glance.

Say the word if the block is also too much and I'll ship B — the model underneath is identical either way, and the difference is about ten lines of view code.

Unchanged

Rank is money at stake: min(allowance, balance) × price, so an unlimited grant reaches your whole balance and the number correctly falls to nothing when you spend the token. An operator grant (the Doodles line) has no amount and this app prices no NFT, so it's stated rather than counted as $0 — which would sort the most dangerous grant last. Ages come from Thing.grantedAt and stay silent when no real block time was read. The button opens the existing ApprovalPrepareCard; nothing here signs.
