The price object

The receipt pass replaced nine blocks with one piece of paper. The price surface never had that pass: it is still seven independently-padded blocks under a text title, with no container, no subject and nothing the app actually says. This is what the same treatment looks like here — and the one thing a price has that a receipt doesn't.

The change

Five labels, or one object

On the left, what ships today. The sheet title says the name, the eyebrow says how long ago you saved the row, the pill says its window, the chips say the same window again, and the spec table says the site. Five labels and no object — which is the exact failure the receipt's own doc names (“three of those blocks were labels for other blocks”).

On the right, the same facts as one card: a subject that carries the asset, a name, the figure, a freshness stamp, a sentence, and the chart underneath as its evidence.

The half that's new

A price that says something

This is the real difference, and it is the thing the receipt already has that the price surface has no version of. A money row earns a second card by making a claim and putting the chart under it as evidence — “Your fourth send to maria.eth” over the bars.

Every sentence below is arithmetic on the closes array we already fetch, plus the watch anchor already stored on the thing. Nothing is inferred, nothing is predicted, and none of it needs a model or another request.

States

The stamp is freshness, not finality

This is the one place the price object must diverge from the receipt. A receipt's stamp says whether it is finished, and its edge tears when it is. A price is never finished — so the slot carries the only state a price really has, which is how recently we read it. That is also the rail that just shipped: past two minutes the halo goes and the object says so.

Cost

What it would take

The object itself is a view and a `Shape`-free container — no new field, no request, no CloudKit deploy. The sentence is a new Foundation-only type beside MoneyCommentary, which means the harness can compile it whole, which is the only way a sentence in this app is allowed to exist.

Sheet hero branch + the title stands downhalf a day
Stock and token on one grammarfolded in — they stop diverging by accident

Specimens are HTML standing in for SwiftUI; the curves are drawn from a fixed series rather than a live fetch, and the asset mark is a stand-in for the bundled artwork AssetMark would resolve. Every colour, radius, type size and spacing value is the shipped token.
