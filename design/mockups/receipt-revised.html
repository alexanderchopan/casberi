Receipt, revised

The receipt already does the hard part. It is one object rather than a labelled stack, and it carries finality in the shape of the paper — which is a more Apple idea than most things Apple ships. Five changes would finish it. Every specimen below is drawn from the real values in DesignTokens.swift, at the real sizes.

The tear should be audible

Torn means final; flat means the paper is still in the machine. It is the sharpest idea on the card and VoiceOver never hears it — the receipt reads out as eight loose fragments, not one of which mentions finality.

Make the card a single accessibility element whose value carries the state, and give the strip an AXChartDescriptor so the history plays as an Audio Graph instead of sitting there as decoration.

Becoming final is a moment

The sheet already recomposes when the Safe queue read lands, which means somebody can be looking at the paper at the instant it tears. Today it swaps. Apple Pay's whole identity is that instant — the mark, the haptic, the sound.

3 of 5

A flat edge belongs in the Island

If the paper is still in the machine, the machine should be visible without opening the app. A pending authorization, a deposit in screening, an unsigned Safe transaction — each is a bounded state with a known endpoint, which is exactly the shape ActivityKit wants.

You already have the widget target, the payload contract and two Live Activities. It also fits the three-lifetimes rule cleanly: a pending record is a reading with a shelf life, and this one expires by tearing.

4 of 5

The face is a door

In Apple Wallet you tap the merchant and get your history with them. This sheet has that history — it is drawn eighty points further down — but the disc is inert, and naming lives at the bottom in the dial.

Make the subject and the corner pip open the address card. It is the gesture people try first, which is exactly why the framed screenshot became tappable in the first place. Keep the dial at parity so nothing is only reachable by a tap on a picture.

5 of 5

The figure is a number, not a string

amount.number is preformatted text, so the locale never gets to decide the separators — and a pending amount that changes simply blinks to the new one.

Push a FormatStyle down into the view and give the figure numericText. An authorization that settles higher then counts to its new value, which is the one place on this card where a change deserves to be watchable.

One to refuse

Frosted paper is the wrong paper

Apple would reach for a material here — a frosted surface with vibrant ink, the environment showing through. It is tempting, because the card's whole metaphor is paper and the pour is you hand-painting the very thing a material gives you for free.

Keep the pour. Translucency is a semantic claim in Apple's own grammar: it means chrome hovering over content you can still scroll underneath. The receipt is not hovering over anything — it is the record. Watch what happens to the figure when the background changes.

On the material card, how legible the figure is depends on what happens to be behind the sheet. For decoration that is charming. For a money figure it is the one place you never want legibility to be weather. And a frosted edge is a soft edge, so the torn silhouette — your only glanceable state signal — goes mushy. The pour keeps the same environmental tint but makes it deterministic and informational: the hue names the rail, and it is the same colour every time.

The order I'd take them

One and five are the same afternoon and touch one file. Four is the next cheapest and the most felt. Two is a day and pays for itself the first time somebody watches a Safe transaction settle. Three is a real pass and wants its own ruling in the ledger.
