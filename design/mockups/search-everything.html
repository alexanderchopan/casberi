Search everything: an app that's also a stock, and your tokens

Two test searches: "spotify" (an app you've connected, a stock, a subscription and a mailing list) and "meta" (the company behind Muse, Instagram and Threads, plus an earnings call on your calendar and MetaMask Card in Wallet). Each one is drawn in three layouts, then the token cases. The prices are the demo's sample quotes (CompanyPacks.demoPrices: SPOT 690, META 760), not real prices. The glyphs stand in for SF Symbols, and the letter squares stand in for app icons and company logos.

Recommendation: C, by category
  • Why C. The tray is already a list of categories with apps under them, and Home is by category. Search that groups the same way reads as the same tray, filtered. A asks you to learn data types. B gets better when there's an exact name, but needs a second layout for when there isn't one (as with "meta").
  • Order. First the place you opened from (as §1171 does now), then the category holding an exact name, then dock order (CategoryOrder.current).
  • Inside a category: names (apps, accounts, people, lists), then money rows (holdings, stocks, subscriptions, with the figure at the trailing edge), then up to two things. Four rows at most. A category with more ends in "N more in Reading", which opens Find scoped to that category.
  • Apps to add stay in their category with Add at the trailing edge, so Threads sits under Social instead of a separate Add section at the bottom.
  • Find is still the last row, and still searches every word.

Already built, just not wired to the tray

  • Company search by name, ticker, or an app it makes: MarketsIndex.search + CompanyPacks (Muse → Meta, Spotify → SPOT).
  • Token names ↔ tickers: TokenWatch.householdNames. Holdings: HoldingsCache.
  • One identity per service (plan, app, mailing list): ServiceIdentity.
  • Things ranked by words and meaning: Retriever.find, the engine behind Find.

Engineering that comes with it

  • Search stops running in body on every keystroke. It waits about 150ms after the last key, ranks off the main actor, and only the things search uses the embedding model, capped.
  • Pasted text is recognised first: an address, a tx hash or a link.
  • Close-name matching for typos, used only when nothing matches exactly.
