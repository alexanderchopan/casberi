Reaching an app's settings, once its room is gone

Today each app has its own room, and the sliders button beside the room's name opens that app's settings page (§1033). Once Safe, Privy, the cards and the exchanges live inside one Wallet, that button no longer points at one app. Here are four ways to answer it. The settings page itself doesn't change; only the way you reach it does. The same answer would carry to every room we merge later, Work's GitHub included.

AThe sliders button follows the account dropdown

The dropdown under the buttons picks an account. Pick Treasury Safe and the sliders button opens Safe's settings. With "All accounts" picked, it opens a short list of the money apps you've connected.

Good: settings stay inside the room, one button, and it works the same in every room. Cost: one button does two different things depending on the dropdown, which is easy to miss.

BThe tray's app circles open settings (recommended)

The tray already lists every room with its connected apps as circles beside it. Today a circle takes you to that app's room. Once those rooms are gone, the circle has nowhere to go, so it opens the app's settings instead. The room's header, its icon and its name, is drawn in blue and opens the room. Blue is used for nothing else in the tray, so blue means "go to the room" and a circle means "settings".

Good: one place for every app's settings, in every room, without inventing anything new. Every circle keeps doing something once its room is gone. The room keeps its sliders button for room-wide things. Cost: the tray stays, and a circle now does something different from what it does today.

DThe tray keeps its doors and lists rooms, with no app circles

The tray keeps Home, Notes, Apps and Settings, and below them one tile per room. The app circles go, because the account dropdown inside each room already picks an app. Settings then work as in A, from the sliders button.

Good: the tray gets shorter and calmer, and it can't get crowded as you connect more apps. Cost: settings depend on A's two-meaning sliders button, and you can no longer see at a glance which apps you've connected.

CNo tray

With eleven rooms instead of dozens (the nine categories, plus Frames and Logos), you could swipe between them and drop the tray altogether. Settings would then work as in A, from the sliders button.

Good: the least to learn. Cost: reaching the far end is ten swipes, and Apps, Notes and Settings lose the one-tap doors they have at the top of the tray.
