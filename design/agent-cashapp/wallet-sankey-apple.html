Casberi · where the sankey goes

Under the holdings, and it answers a different question

Where: directly beneath the holdings treemap and above lending. The pairing is the argument — the treemap says what you hold (composition, frozen), the sankey says where it went (flow, over a window). Apple always sets a static breakdown next to its movement (Health: today's rings, then the trend; Stocks: the quote, then the chart), and never the reverse order, because you can't read a flow until you know what's flowing.

How: Apple would not ship a raw sankey — the closest they get is Screen Time's stacked areas. So it keeps the diagram but takes Apple's rules: the reading first in big type, ribbons muted and rounded with a single accent for the lane that dominates, and only the lanes thick enough to label carry words. Everything else is a press away — which this figure already does.

The card's anatomy is the same three lines every other card now has — glyph + word, one reading big ("$1,240 in 7 days"), one quiet line — and the diagram is the miniature, just a tall one. That consistency is the point: the sankey stops being a special exhibit and becomes one more measurement card whose picture happens to be a flow.

The NFTs break the rule on purpose, and Apple's own grammar says to. Every other card here leads with a reading because its subject is a number. Art has no reading — a floor price is the one this app already refuses (§387: a bid on the thinnest book, moving without you, beside things kept for reasons that aren't the number). So the pictures ARE the reading: four square tiles at full bleed inside the card, no count in big type, the names demoted to the quiet line. It is Photos' grammar, which is what Apple uses whenever the content is images — the picture never sits beside a bigger number describing it. Position is §387's own ruling, unchanged: under the holdings pair, because "what you hold that's fungible" and "what you hold that isn't" are one question asked twice, and before the room turns to risk.

The press already exists (§386d): a lane names itself in the reading's own slot for 2.5s, then reverts. Apple's addition is that the other lanes recede while it does — so the answer is legible in the diagram, not only in the words.

Two honesty rules the redraw keeps: a lane too thin to carry its name carries nothing — never a shrunken label, never a leader line (it's a press away, which is exactly what §386d built the press for). And an unnamed counterparty still says "an address we can't name" rather than a hex string. What's new: only the largest lane gets the green; every other ribbon is neutral grey, so "where most of it went" is answered before you read a word.
