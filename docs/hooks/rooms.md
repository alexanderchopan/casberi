- **The Markets fold (2026-08-10, user ruling, `Model/MarketsRoom.swift`) — HISTORY: the Markets category is DELETED (user ruling 2026-09-06, prd §638: "i want to get away from crypto bullshit but wallets and the other stuff in them are important"). `MarketsRoom.swift` is deleted, Tokens/L2BEAT/OpenSea fold into Wallet (whose groups now span `NFTs`), and Kalshi, Polymarket, Stocktwits, GeckoTerminal, Circle x402, 1Claw and Open Food Facts are retired seats (`Corpus.retiredSources` — bridge files kept one release, no catalog entry, no chip, no room, no deep link). Everything below describes the mechanism as it shipped and generalized into `CategoryFold` (§351), which is what every folder still runs on.** The seven market seats (Tokens, Kalshi, Polymarket, Stocktwits, GeckoTerminal, Circle x402, OpenSea) share ONE chip once ≥2 are present, and the room behind it carries a venue switcher (`Screens/MarketsVenueSwitcher.swift`). **They are distinct lists nobody wants merged** — a token's 24h percent, a stock's session percent and a market's probability points do not convert, which is why `TokensAsk` and `MarketsAsk` are two composers and why "Busiest" already refuses to cross-merge Kalshi and Polymarket — **but they are lists somebody wants to move BETWEEN**, and icon-only chips scattered through a learned-order strip make that a hunt (two market marks are literally the same letter in a circle). So the aggregate is a SWITCHER, not a composition, and there is deliberately no "All" scope. **THE CENTRAL INVARIANT: "Markets" is a chip LABEL, never a source.** `FeedFilter.source` holds a real seat at all times — `MainSurface.go(to:)` is the one place a label resolves to a venue — so nothing downstream ever sees the string: no new `@Query` shape, no `FeedScreen.Shape` case, no `Corpus.earnsRoom` exception, no deep-link change, and every market room renders exactly as before. (Contrast `Pinboard.room`, which HAD to be a sentinel because its rows are selected by `pinnedAt`; these rows already belong to seats.) Membership is derived from the catalog's own Markets category (which spans the `Markets` AND `NFTs` groups), so a new seat joins the day it lands. The fold takes the position of the best-ranked member it replaces, so `ChipMemory` still decides where the cluster sits and every member's weight is still recorded. **Two orders, each where it belongs**: `ShellChrome.marketVenues` is published in LEARNED order because `MarketsRoom.landing` takes its first entry as the fallback (catalog order there opens the chip on Tokens for someone who lives in Kalshi — "the fold costs no extra tap" failing on the very first tap), while the switcher re-sorts to CATALOG order for display, because a four-word capsule has not earned learned order and one that reshuffles between opens reads as broken. `MarketsRoom.landing` remembers the last venue, so the chip reopens where you left off. **The switcher is PINNED, not a List section** — `walletSwitcherBar`'s 2026-07-20 ruling (prd §136), which bites harder here: as a section it scrolls away with the room it names and its glass blurs nothing, and this one says which ROOM you're in rather than scoping one room's rows. It shares that `safeAreaInset` because Wallet is not a member, so the two can never both render. **`ShellChrome` publishes TWO lists and the split is load-bearing**: `chipOrder` is what the strip SHOWS (folded), so Mac's ⌘1–⌘9 stays positional against what you can see and resolves the label before writing; `sourceOrder` is unfolded and is what anything that WRITES a source takes — the sources tray above all, since it is the one screen whose own doc says it "claims to show every source". Fed the folded list it dropped all seven seats and grew a "Markets" cell writing a non-source into `FeedFilter.source`; both leaks shipped in this feature's first cut and are now guarded mechanically. **Three things that would fail INVISIBLY and are handled**: the catch-bob/coin-flip key on `MarketsRoom.chipLabel(for:)`, or every market arrival celebration fires against a chip not in the strip; the attention ring answers for all the seats behind the fold, or a broken Kalshi has no ring anywhere; and `step(_:)` walks CHIPS, appending the active one when the room you're in has no chip (the guarantee the now-deleted `feedLabels` used to make for the retired pager). **Wallet is deliberately NOT folded this way** — Aave/Morpho/Peer/Safe are lenses on ONE subject (your addresses) and compose into a single room with a single balance, so they never had chips to fold; five venues with five separate watchlists cannot compose into one reading. **The cross-venue twin was UN-GATED in the same pass** (`PredictionDisagreement`, `PredictionBrowseSection`): it required the merged `.all` scope, which the fold retires, so left alone the one reading an aggregate produces that neither venue can produce alone would have silently disappeared. It now draws in every scope, `findReverse` covers a Polymarket room (where no Kalshi row has been fetched), and `BrowseCard.twinVenue` names whose price it is — the mark was hardcoded to Polymarket, harmless only while Kalshi cards alone had twins. **`findReverse` reads Kalshi's book ONCE with an empty query and matches locally, and its first cut — mirroring `find` with the arguments swapped — could never have matched anything**: `PolymarketBridge.search` is a real server-side relevance search, while `KalshiWatch.book`'s phase 1 is `hay.contains(q)`, a literal substring test of the WHOLE query, and a full Polymarket question is never a verbatim substring of a Kalshi title. Six awaits that always answered `[]`, with the room rendering perfectly and the doc claiming the comparison survived. The fix is also cheaper (one cached read, not six sequential). **The twin read runs AFTER the book is committed**, or un-gating it turns six sequential searches into something that holds the skeleton up on every room load; `twinPrices` is not cleared before the read (stale keys can only match a market still on screen, and clearing blinks every bar off) but IS cleared when the comparison stops being possible at all. Note `.all` is now unreachable by construction — both venues connected implies ≥2 members implies the fold — so `PredictionVenueSwitcher` and the `scope == .all` branches are dormant rather than deleted. `PredictionRoomBook`'s own switcher stands down under the fold, so there is never a switcher inside a switcher. Every `MainSurface` mount NSLogs `marketsFold|` (folded / venue list / landing / member count) — an unfolded strip has three causes that look identical from outside, and one of them is the catalog category being renamed out from under `members`. Guarded by `scripts/markets-fold-selftest.sh` (in `verify.sh`), which compiles `MarketsRoom.swift` WHOLE and unmodified against an inert catalog stub — 22 assertions, 9 mutations, plus a drift guard against the REAL catalog, since the stub can prove the derivation but never that a Markets category still exists to derive from.

- `-topicMapProbe <YES|Source>` — the **text treemap** (prd §230, 2026-07-30; §247 widened it past Photos; §283 added Files), headless. Bare `YES` means Photos; `-topicMapProbe Instagram` maps an export's own captions/comments; `-topicMapProbe Files` maps a connected folder's images' OCR ("What your images say" — the subtitle counts ONLY image files, since PDFs/text files share the `.file` kind and the map never reads them; membership is `FilesIngest.isImageRef`, shared with the grid test and the heal). **Files is the app's second MIXED room (prd §283, 2026-08-02):** healed images lead as a photo grid, PDFs/text files/unhealed images as rows — before this the room shared the `.notes` shape (`ExcerptRow` — title + excerpt text + time, no image) and a folder of screenshots rendered as pure text rows with their thumbnails stored but never drawn ("What you write about", `.note` rows only — the room's other half is somebody else's writing — with the import receipt excluded, and no OCR to wait for since the text lands with the row). Originally: the Photos feed's OCR treemap. Runs the `ocrTopics` backfill first (`ScreenshotTopics.healTopics` reads the OCR `content` already on each shot — no PHAsset walk, so it's cheap and self-terminating once `topicsAt` is stamped on every screenshot), then composes `FeedInsight.topicMap` and NSLogs `topicMapCard|` + one `topicMapCell|` per ranked cell + a sample of `topicMapTerms|` per-shot extractions (one NSLog per line — the `-todayProbe` truncation lesson). The treemap (`TopicMapHero`) replaces the generic "Your capture year" heatmap on Photos, but `FeedHeatmap` still registers Photos so a wordless/thin library falls back to the heatmap (topicMap returns nil under 6 shots-with-terms or <2 recurring terms). Terms are DETERMINISTIC (`ScreenshotTopics.terms`: domains + NLTagger org/place/person, UI-chrome stoplist) so every cell label literally appears in a screenshot — the pure `terms`/`cells` logic is `swift`-harness-tested (domains vs version strings, recurrence gate, single-assignment tie-break, determinism), not sim-verified. Two additive `Thing` fields (`ocrTopics: [String]`, `topicsAt: Date?`), no migration.

- `-roomInsightProbe <Source>` — what a source's room LEADS with (prd §247, 2026-07-31). Asks every hero card independently (**the per-source heads** — Cloudflare's `runway`, `stripeHead`, `posthogHead` — then `anniversary` → `topicMap` → `leaderboard` → `distribution` → `mosaic` → `heatmap`), NSLogs each one's own answer (`roomInsight|` per card, plus `roomInsightCell|`/`roomInsightRow|` for the contents) and names the winner. **The ORDER lives in `FeedScreen.shapedSections` and this mirrors it — change one, change both.** It exists because the failure mode here isn't a card computing wrongly, it's one card silently OWNING the slot another was built for: §219's social roster shipped and never rendered for weeks because the Farcaster/Bluesky heatmaps outranked it. **The heatmap moved to LAST in that chain on 2026-07-31** (it answers WHEN, the weakest lead, and its static registry means it can never decline the slot) — safe because the heatmap registry and the leaderboard/distribution/mosaic source lists don't intersect at a single name, so no pre-existing room changed. Live state (a Twitch stream, a social roster) is view-level, outranks everything, and is NOT read by this probe — it says so in its own first line. **The per-source heads share rank 1 and are grouped into ONE term in `shapedSections` (`sourceHead`, prd §298, 2026-08-04)** — each claims exactly one source so they can never compete, and each gate below re-states its predecessors by hand, so three separate `let`s would mean editing five gates to add a card and mis-ranking it if you missed one. They exist because `FeedInsight` is pure over `[Thing]` by contract: a certificate expiry, a Stripe balance and a PostHog reading are bridge state, not corpus, so no registry can reach them.

- `-stripeRoomProbe YES` / `-posthogRoomProbe YES` — the two room heads' readings line by line (prd §298, 2026-08-04; one NSLog per line, the `-todayProbe` truncation lesson). Both compose the REAL card off landed rows plus stored bridge state and spend NOTHING — no request, no new `Thing` field, no CloudKit deploy. Each exists because an empty head has several causes that render as one nothing and only some are bugs: for Stripe, not connected / a balance never read on this device (a fresh install syncs rows but NOT UserDefaults) / no row carrying a `dueAt` / every deadline outside the window; for PostHog, nothing watched / readings never fetched here. Pair `-posthogRoomProbe` with `-posthogSeed "<event>:<c,c,c>[|total]"` to verify the whole card — discs, ring, silence ordering — with no account and no network. - **The note THING SHEETS, past §366 (prd §399, 2026-08-17).** §366 gave the category its three anatomies (a dated entry, a named vault note, a marked passage) and fixed the worst of it; this is the residue, plus one item §398 created the same afternoon. **(1) The photograph nothing drew** — §398 landed both journal exports' pictures and no view rendered them, so an entry's own photograph appeared in exactly ONE place in the app: as a 92pt tile on a DIFFERENT entry's "That day" shelf. `NoteEntryPhoto` sits below the dateline and above the prose (the date identifies the entry, the picture is part of what it says), drawn by `PhotoWell` and never a hand-rolled `Image` — that is the one image view here honouring `redactionReasons`, and a private photograph at 220pt surviving into the app-switcher snapshot is the leak that guard exists for. The ROW got a 40pt trailing thumb, deliberately NOT a grid tile: every mixed room in this app promotes a WORDLESS picture into a grid and keeps a captioned one as a row, and a journal entry always has words. **(2) The same date in other years** — the room's anniversary answers TODAY's date, this answers the ENTRY's, which is what a journal is opened for. Bounded by construction: a `#Predicate` cannot call a calendar, so the alternative is fetching the room and filtering (the corpus walk this project forbids); each candidate year is a narrow range read with `fetchLimit = 1`, capped at `yearSpan` (12), scoped to ONE source (merging two exports of the same years gives two rows for one day with no way to tell which journal). **(3) Previous/next** — a journal is a SEQUENCE and the sheet dead-ended, so reading three consecutive days meant sheet-close-scroll-sheet three times. Two reads at `fetchLimit = 2` (two, not one, so an import receipt can't be offered as the next entry — a door onto our own note about a sync, from inside somebody's diary); older left, newer right, each door naming its entry's DATE rather than "Previous", and a missing neighbour drawing NOTHING rather than a disabled control. **(4) Markdown is drawn as markdown, and only where it IS markdown** — `NoteProse` set the whole body as one `Text`, so a Day One or Obsidian note drew its own punctuation (`# Monday` as a hash and a word, `**finally**` with the asterisks in). `NoteSheet.blocks` splits four shapes and no more; **`markdownSources` is a per-source FACT, not a preference** (Day One escapes markdown punctuation on the way out — `unescapeMarkdown` exists because of it — and a vault is markdown by definition, while Apple Journal's body is HTML through `plainText(fromHTML:)` and a `You` note is whatever somebody typed). **Blank lines separate blocks; single newlines inside a paragraph are KEPT** — the knowing departure from CommonMark, because a journal's line breaks are the writer's. **Every marker requires a space after it**, which is the whole of its correctness (`#hashtag` is Obsidian's own inline tag, `-5 degrees` is not a bullet), and the fold cuts at a BLOCK boundary keeping at least one, where the old `lineLimit(12)` cut mid-sentence at whatever width the device happened to be. **Wikilinks became walkable**: extracted at ingest since 2026-07-28 and listed UNDER the note, they still read as literal double brackets in the body — the one place a vault user expects a link to work. `markdownWithWikilinks` rewrites them under a **`casberi-note://` scheme, deliberately NOT `casberi://`** (that is the app's real deep-link scheme and `RootShell.route` acts on it, so a note holding a crafted link could otherwise reach a router); the alias split mirrors `NoteLinks.extract` exactly or the body draws a link the shelf doesn't list. **(5) A shared Apple Note gets the anatomy** — §366 left `You` out pending "an answer to what `You` means (wrote it, or merely brought it)", so those rows had NO anatomy and drew a title cut from the note's first line and then the same line again beneath it. The answer is that we cannot know and it does not matter: `Act.kept` claims neither ("you kept it", at that time, on this device), and the `.device` sentence follows the ACT rather than the origin ("Kept here", not "Recorded here", which only a voice note earns). Scoped by KIND — `You` holds every hand capture, so a `.note` gets it and a saved `.link` keeps the generic sheet — and NOT added to `NoteSheetSource.sources`, which the harness checks against the catalog's Notes group (`You` is not a seat). One consequence: the "That day" shelf excluded the same SOURCE, identical to same-kind for a journal and for `You` hiding that day's screenshots, links and voice notes — the rule is same source AND same kind now, filtered in Swift because a `#Predicate` on `kind` is the transformable-attribute crash class this project has a report for. Guarded by `scripts/note-sheet-selftest.sh` (compiles `NoteSheet.swift` WHOLE; +40 assertions, +14 mutations, +16 drift guards). Three of its own cases were "right result for the wrong reason": a `]` in a wikilink alias can never reach the escaper (the capture is `[^\]]+`), the fold's keep-one guard is invisible at the shipped limit and only load-bearing at 0, and a `Voice` fixture that omitted the act silently exercised the new default.

- `-journalRoomProbe YES` — the two JOURNAL room heads, year by year (prd §398, 2026-08-17, `Model/JournalRoom.swift` + `JournalRoomSource.swift`, card `Screens/JournalRoomCard.swift`). Both journals in ONE launch, named apart, one NSLog per year (the `-todayProbe` truncation lesson). **Day One and Apple Journal led with `FeedHeatmap`'s trailing-twelve-month grid from the day they shipped** — over a corpus whose whole point is that it goes back further, so a journal kept since 2019 was charted for its last year and a journal kept and then stopped was an empty square over a decade. The head is the SPAN (silent years drawn, never skipped — the gap is the reading and dropping it rescales the axis), the longest run of consecutive days as the headline, and the fullest years as rows carrying each year's own subject. **Costs nothing**: `capturedAt`/`ocrTopics`/`sourceRef`, no request, no new `Thing` field, no CloudKit deploy. **Why the RUN leads**: every other room records things that ARRIVED, a journal records showing up — below `streakFloor` (3) there is nothing worth naming and the fullest year leads instead, since two consecutive days is a weekend. **§349's rule is why the year rows carry subjects** — this displaces `FeedInsight.topicMap`, which the same pass gave these rooms, so it says which year was which rather than what eight years were about at once. **Two floors and the second is in DAYS**: `minimumYears` is 2 (a journal is a fraction of an archive's volume) but two calendar years is also what six weeks across New Year's Eve has, so `minimumSpanDays` (180) sits beside it — a year count can be doubled by an accident of the calendar, a day count cannot. **Obsidian is deliberately NOT one of these rooms**: a vault note's `capturedAt` is the file's MTIME, so an edit moves a 2019 note to today and the strip would chart when files were touched while claiming to chart when somebody wrote (§83 where it would look most convincing); guarded as a NEGATIVE grep on a comment-stripped copy, since the source explains the exclusion by naming it (the Obsidian/Cursor lesson, fourth time). **ONE `SourceHead` case, TWO probe labels** (`dayOneHead`/`appleJournalHead`) — `verify.sh`'s room-head coverage is a zsh associative array keyed by that name, so one shared label could only ever assert that ONE of the two journals composes, and they have separate corpora and separate demo seeds. The demo's journals were 12 and 8 entries in one season, which would have made this head correctly decline and the coverage check read the decline as a gap (§375's X lesson, two rooms over) — `DemoSeedAll.writing()` now spans three calendar years each, opening on a real run. **The ANNIVERSARY outranks it** (`OnThisDay`, promoted above `sourceHead` in the same pass): a card about how many years you kept a journal must not cover what you wrote on this date in one of them, and it is nil on nearly every day so it takes the head rarely. That promotion is safe only because it is SCOPED to the memories room and these two, none of which has an alarm head — widen it to a room whose head is a dispute deadline and a nostalgia card covers something time-critical. `OnThisDayHero` gained a TEXT treatment with it, which also closed a hole: it was pixels-or-nothing, and since the anniversary SUPPRESSES every card below it, a text echo drew an empty head slot on exactly the days the room had something to say. **UNMEASURED** — no Day One or Apple Journal export has ever been held by this project — so `scripts/journal-room-selftest.sh` (Foundation-only, compiled WHOLE, 70 assertions, 12 mutations, 15 drift guards, in `verify.sh`) is not the best proof these numbers are right but the ONLY one, a degree stronger than the Stripe/PostHog harnesses which could at least be checked by anyone willing to mint a key. Two of its OWN fixtures were wrong on the first run, both the "right result for the wrong reason" class: the minimum-entries case sat one entry ABOVE the floor it tested, and the no-silent-years case used 2024 and 2026, which leaves 2025 silent and could never have taken the branch it was written for — both now assert the fixture's own premise first. A third case was DELETED rather than fixed (`longestRun` takes a `Set<Int>`, so a duplicate day cannot reach it: the type is the guard, and a stronger one than an assertion).

- **The reading rooms, past §312 (prd §455, 2026-08-23).** Three of the four items are a SURFACE catching up with data the app had already gone and got — the §397 shape — so read §312 before touching any of it. `Model/FeedRoomHealth.swift` (Foundation-only), `FeedRoomHealthSource.swift`, `Screens/ArticleBody.swift`, plus `FeedArticleText.readableURL`/`fetchOnOpen`, `FeedInsight.Leaderboard.Scope` and `FeedScreen.readingScope`.
  - **THE ARTICLE IS FETCHED WHEN YOU OPEN IT.** `FeedArticleText.sweep` is bounded like the background pass it is — `perPass` 6 rows per foreground, a 30-day `window`, abandoned after `maxAttempts` — so on any corpus with several feeds MOST stories still had no body when opened, the sheet fell back to a link preview card, and the only way to read the piece was to leave for the browser. `fetchOnOpen` drops THREE of those bounds deliberately (the window: you opened it; `maxAttempts`: a person re-opening is not a robot re-asking; `perPass`/`pace`: one open is one request) and **still WRITES the ledger while never READING it**, so a paywalled article opened twice teaches the sweep to leave it alone without the person being told no. **`readableURL` is the ONE eligibility rule, shared** — the two paths must disagree about WHEN and never about WHAT, or a tap downloads a podcast's whole audio enclosure to read its first 512KB as text.
  - **The board narrows its own room, and the BOARD DOES NOT NARROW.** `Leaderboard.scope` carries the FIELD it ranked (`publisher` = `authorHandle`, `writer` = `postAuthor`) because RSS and Substack pick between `bylines` and `counted` AT RUNTIME depending on what their feeds name — a scope assuming one field narrows to nothing on exactly the corpora that took the other branch. `roomScoped(_:narrowingToPublisher:)` is passed false by `recomputeHeads` and nothing else: a board recomputed over one publisher is one bar naming the choice you already made, with no way back (§357's venue-switcher rule, and the deliberate difference from the x402 lane strip, which narrows its own head because there the control survives separately). Tapping the scoped row CLEARS it. The scope is `@State` (dies with the room, `x402Lane`'s lifetime, deliberately not §356's shell-held shape) and is in `headIdentity`.
  - **A feed that stopped answering says so IN THE ROOM.** `FeedFreshness.trouble(for:)` could say it since §312 and said it only on the setup screen nobody revisits. Silent by default; NEVER says a feed is gone (a status code cannot support that, and YouTube answers a throttled client a plain 404); one feed is NAMED with its own observation, several are COUNTED because their reasons differ; the count is of TROUBLED feeds, never followed ones. **Above the head deliberately** — it says whether the room is COMPLETE, and every reading below it is computed over rows a dead feed stopped contributing to. A DOOR (`route.pushBridge`), not a label. **Stated ceiling: a follow whose feed URL was never resolved is EXCLUDED**, not counted as troubled — `FeedFreshness` is keyed by URL, so there is no observation to report.
  - **LISTEN** (`ArticleSpeech`) — on-device `AVSpeechSynthesizer` over the fetched body, no request, no key, nothing in `NetworkReach` (guarded as a negative). Deliberately NOT a `Verb`: that file caps at three discs and this is conditional on whether a body landed, so a disc would be present-and-dead on every story the fetch missed. ONE synthesizer app-wide (a per-view one means two articles reading at once from a second window); `speakingID` is what lets a button know the voice is its own. `ArticleListenButton` takes an ID and a String, never the `Thing` — the liveness audit's check 5 flagged the first shape, and the type is the better guard.
  - **DECLINED: read/unread state.** The feed is a corpus, not an inbox; a room that grades you on what you have not opened is a different product.
  - `-feedHealthProbe YES` now also prints one `feedRoomHealth|` line per reading room (feeds, resolved, quiet, the line it draws). It reports per ROOM rather than leaving it to be inferred from the per-URL census, because the two legitimately disagree: a failing record for an UNFOLLOWED feed appears in the census and must never reach a room, and `feeds=3 quiet=0` beside a census full of failures is CORRECT when the follows are unresolved.
  - `scripts/feed-reading-selftest.sh` (in `verify.sh`) compiles `FeedRoomHealth` WHOLE — 20 assertions, 7 mutations, drift guards for all three halves. **It found dead code in its own subject on the first run**: a `firstReason` accumulator whose first-vs-last mutation SURVIVED, because a reason is only printed when exactly one feed is troubled, so the two are identical by construction and no fixture could ever have separated them. Fixed by deleting the question (name and reason are one pair) rather than by writing a cleverer fixture. A second fixture was two-troubled-of-two, which cannot tell a count of failures from a count of follows — three feeds now, two troubled. **UNMEASURED**: no publisher has been fetched on a tap from this host, and the Listen voice has never spoken.
