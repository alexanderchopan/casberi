- **The address book's delight pass (prd §441, 2026-08-22):** five moments and three walks, all guarded by `address-book-selftest.sh`'s drift half. **The star flight** (`Screens/AddressFlight.swift`) sends a wallet's face from its book row to its shelf slot — the moment §212 claimed in prose ("tapping a star visibly drops the wallet into the shelf") and never built. An `overlayPreferenceValue` and NOT `matchedGeometryEffect`, because that modifier wants exactly one source and here BOTH views persist (starring does not remove the row); its endpoints are anchors published by the views, its SIZE comes off the ramp (`DS.Face.list` → `DS.Face.shelf`) rather than off those anchor rects — which are layout frames and stop matching the face the moment a mark gains a border, a real defect `face-ramp-audit` caught — and it ARCS, since both points sit in the same column and a straight interpolation reads as scrolling. **Two traps paid for:** the launch is deferred one runloop turn (the slot does not exist until the write has been observed, so a same-turn publish gives the overlay a `from` and no `to`, which draws nothing and reads as the feature being absent), and the anchor key is `AddressBook.key(for:)` and not the raw address (a watch may be stored as "vitalik.eth" while the book holds the resolved form, so a raw key leaves the two anchors unable to find each other on exactly the wallets people bother to name). Plus: a dropped face is ABSORBED by the group deck (a one-shot flag, never a membership diff — the book is `@Observable` and has already re-rendered by the time anything could compare); a tapped spine node LIGHTS its own ribbons for 240ms before the sheet covers them (§295's same-weight ruling intact — it is a transient answer to a gesture, not a claim about which relationship matters); a newly-seen connection draws DASHED via `AddressConnectionsSeen`, which is the one part of the sky worth keeping, seeds silently on first sight and lives in UserDefaults because "have you looked at this" is a fact about THIS DEVICE'S SCREEN; and the scrubber floats a large letter to the leading side while dragging. **Three costs**: `book.search` ran FOUR times per body pass (sections, id map, header count, scrubber) and is now one `let` threaded down — not a cache, since the book is `@Observable` and a pass only happens when something really changed; the manager fetched the corpus TWICE in one `onAppear` (`AddressActivity` reads Wallet + Peer + Privacy Pools, a strict superset of `AddressConnections`' Wallet) and now walks once via `AddressActivity.relevant(in:)`; and **`edges(from:)` re-sorts rather than trusting its caller**, which is load-bearing — `AddressActivity` hands back NEWEST first and node order is FIRST-DEALT by §295, so taking the given order silently reverses the spine into a card that renders perfectly and lists the newest relationship as the oldest. UNPROVEN on a device: every one of the five is gesture-driven and no static check can exercise them.

- **The Safe CO-SIGNER — a key that can sign and can never spend (2026-08-21, prd §425 + §426, `docs/signer-spec.md`).** `Model/SafeTransaction.swift` (the EIP-712 arithmetic, Foundation-only), `Model/SignerKey.swift` (the key), `Model/SafeSigner.swift` (the refusals, the rail, the one write), `Screens/SafeQueueCard.swift`'s `SafeSignBlock` (the ask), `Screens/SafeScreen.swift` (setup). A secp256k1 key born on this phone becomes one owner of a Safe whose threshold is ≥2, so "cannot spend" stops being a policy we promise and becomes arithmetic in a contract we do not control. **`-signerProbe YES`** — key/address/biometry, then every pending Safe row's verdict, one NSLog each (the `-todayProbe` truncation lesson); it SIGNS NOTHING and must never raise a biometric prompt, or it is a probe nobody can put in a sweep. It exists because "no Sign button" has SEVEN causes that render as one blank space and only two are bugs (no key, no pending row, an unsignable chain, threshold 1, not an owner, the chain didn't answer, the hashes disagree) — and the last is the only one that means something is WRONG rather than not ready.
  - **NEVER SIGN A HASH CASBERI COMPUTED ALONE.** Before signing, `eth_call` `getTransactionHash` (`0xd8d11f78`) on the Safe itself and require agreement; refuse on mismatch AND on failure to read. That one call turns every possible encoding mistake into a DECLINE instead of a valid signature over a different transaction, and handles Safe version drift for free (a pre-1.3.0 Safe uses a different EIP-712 domain, disagrees, and is refused with no version table to maintain). The local encoder still exists because it is what the remote answer is checked *against* — a lone number from whichever public RPC answered is not evidence.
  - **The failure class is invisible to everything else in this repo.** A wrong domain separator, type hash, ABI padding or an INLINED `data` field produces a signature that is well-formed and recovers to a real address. The build is green, the screen is right, the tap is right, and the money leaves. So `scripts/safetx-selftest.sh` (in `verify.sh`) compiles `SafeTransaction.swift` WHOLE and unmodified, pins six fixtures derived by `scripts/support/safetx-vectors.py` **and greps that script's own output to prove they match**, and is mutation-proven twenty-four ways. **The spec's five fixtures were not enough and the harness found that itself**: all five leave `safeTxGas`/`baseGas`/`gasPrice`/`gasToken`/`refundReceiver` at zero, so swapping two of them reproduced every hash and the mutation survived, green. A sixth fixture gives every field a distinct value. Standing rule, third instance: **a fixture only tests the rule it names if it FAILS that rule and passes every other one.**
  - **`v` is a DISCRIMINATOR, not a parity bit.** Safe reads `0` = contract signature, `1` = pre-approved hash, `> 30` = `eth_sign` against the `"\x19Ethereum Signed Message:\n32"`-prefixed hash, and 27/28 = a plain EIP-712 signature over the hash. Casberi signs the `safeTxHash` DIRECTLY, so 27/28 is the only correct answer; emitting 31/32 makes the Safe verify a prefixed hash and recover a stranger. Low-s is ASSERTED, not trusted to libsecp256k1's default.
  - **The curve library is `21-DOT-DEV/swift-secp256k1` pinned to 0.21.1, NOT the newest tag, and the reason is a BUILD property.** From 0.22.0 it assembles `P256K` with a SwiftPM **build-tool plugin**, and `xcodebuild` refuses an unvalidated plugin from the command line — measured 2026-08-21, the Catalyst build dies at `Validate plug-in "SharedSourcesPlugin"` and passes only with `-skipPackagePluginValidation`, which would mean that flag in eight `xcodebuild` sites and plugin fingerprint validation off on every build of the app holding a signing key. 0.21.1 predates the plugin, resolves clean (SwiftPM prunes its ungated dev deps — no SwiftLint/SwiftFormat in `Package.resolved`, measured), and its `signature(for:)` **throws** where 0.23.2 calls `fatalError`. **Trap: downgrading across a traits boundary fails with "Traits […] have been enabled on package that declares no traits" until `Package.resolved` is reverted** — the stale resolve file, not any cache, holds the trait set.
  - **No seed phrase, and that is the security model.** Device-only Keychain, non-synchronizable, `SecAccessControlCreateWithFlags(..., [.biometryCurrentSet])` so every signature costs a Face ID and **re-enrolling Face ID erases the key** — the setup screen spends its one §315 gray sentence on that cost rather than the pitch, because it is the argument for 2-of-3 over 2-of-2. `.privateKeyUsage` from the spec is DROPPED: it governs a Secure Enclave `kSecClassKey`, and this is a raw scalar in a generic password (the Enclave speaks P-256, which a Safe accepts only through a WebAuthn signer contract §425 does not build). **No export path, asserted**: `SecItemCopyMatching` appears exactly ONCE in `SignerKey.swift` and the harness counts OCCURRENCES not lines — `grep -c` counts lines, and a second call appended to the same line survived that guard's first run.
  - **This is the app's FIRST outbound write ever** — one `POST` of one 65-byte signature to `multisig-transactions/<hash>/confirmations/` on Safe's own service, keyless because the signature is its own authorization. Body shape read from the service's OWN source (`SafeMultisigConfirmationSerializer`), not docs prose; the spec's `/safes/{address}/…` path is wrong. Conduct-guarded: any other write verb, any second POST, or any host but `api.safe.global` in that file fails the build, and `NetworkReach` must keep SAYING a signature is sent. If the service refuses it, the signature is NOT thrown away — it goes to the clipboard for tier 0, because a Face ID the person already gave must not be lost to a third party's outage.
  - **Gnosis Chain is refused, deliberately**: `WalletApprovals` carries no Gnosis host, so the rail cannot run there, and a chain where the cross-check cannot run is a chain this app must not sign on. Reading Gnosis Safes is unchanged.
  - **AN N-of-N SAFE CANNOT BE REPAIRED, and that is the one warning this feature owes (prd §427).** `swapOwner`/`removeOwner`/`changeThreshold` are executed BY the Safe on itself, so they need the threshold met like anything else — in a 2-of-2 a single lost key means the Safe can never be signed for again AND never fixed. Money finished. (A recovery MODULE is the only escape; this app can see a module exists and cannot tell a recovery one from any other, so it never counts on it.) **The flag choice cannot carry this**: a device-only key with no seed phrase dies with the phone under any access control, so the hazard is structural and `.biometryCurrentSet` is only a frequency dial on it. So: `SafeSigner.Standing` reads owners+threshold FROM THE CHAIN and states `hasNoSpareOwner` on the setup screen about YOUR Safe — never an all-clear from a read that didn't answer — and **it is never a refusal**, because in a 2-of-2 naming this phone OUR DECLINE IS THE LOCK; the sign block says the fact and signs anyway, guarded by a mutation-proven NEGATIVE grep that no refusal is built from it. `Ready.addsASpareOwner` greets the repair (`addOwnerWithThreshold` that does NOT raise the threshold to match; one that does leaves the Safe just as stuck). Discovery needs `SafeBridge.signerSafes(requireExecuted: false)` — the `nonce > 0` spam filter is right for a watched wallet and wrong for an address WE generated seconds ago (a decoy must name an address that already exists), and a brand-new 2-of-2 has `nonce == 0`.
  - **Re-enrolling Face ID does NOT make a new key** — the key is 32 bytes in the Keychain and the biometric is only the lock on the drawer. Under `.biometryAny` the same bytes, address and Safe seat survive with nothing to re-apply; **`.biometryCurrentSet` is what creates the errand**, by destroying the item. Kept anyway, because a changed enrolled set is a changed AUTHORITY the Safe cannot see. Its cost was invisible until §427: the cached address outlives the item, so the app showed a Sign button that failed with the **same sentence a cancelled prompt gives**. `SignerKey.presence()` splits `.none`/`.present`/`.destroyed` by an **attribute-only** query (no `kSecReturnData`) so it decrypts nothing and raises no prompt; an unreadable keychain reports `.present`, never `.destroyed`, since that sentence sends somebody to arrange an on-chain transaction.
  - **Two guard defects the harness found in ITSELF, one shape:** the no-export guard counted `SecItemCopyMatching`, so `presence()`'s honest attribute-only query read as a second reader of the private bytes (it counts `kSecReturnData` now — the line that actually decrypts); and `prepare`'s chain reads were guarded by a bare `SafeCall.getThresholdSelector` name grep, which kept passing once `standing()` added a second call site, so **the mutation removing the read that GATES SIGNING survived**. Both anchored to `prepare`'s own call sites. Standing rule: a guard must prove the condition is the WHOLE condition, not that the words appear.
  - **UNSIGNED. Nothing has ever been signed against a real Safe.** No simulator has a Secure Enclave gesture and no harness here can make a Safe execute; `docs/signer-spec.md` step 9 (a real 2-of-3 holding trivial funds, on a device) stands.

Pure logic harnessed in `scripts/wallet-viz-selftest.sh` (276 assertions, mutation-proven five ways, incl. per-wallet totals computed over only the DRAWN nodes — a display cap is not an accounting rule). Pair with `-walletAddress` on two launches (the hook fires once per run).

- `-userOpProbe YES` / `-actingPartiesProbe YES` — account abstraction (2026-08-03, prd §293, `Model/WalletUserOps.swift` + `Model/WalletActingParties.swift`). **`-userOpProbe` exists because `WalletGas` was charging smart accounts somebody else's gas**: it took the FULL receipt cost of any transaction that moved tokens out of the wallet, which is right for an EOA and wrong twice over for a smart account — a 4337 bundle's receipt covers several unrelated people's UserOperations (so the wallet was charged for strangers), a paymaster may have paid all of it (real cost zero), and a Safe is executed by an OWNER (so the Safe was charged the owner's gas). Invisible: a running total renders perfectly whatever is in it. **The rule is now "you paid it only if you sent it"** — a `UserOperationEvent` naming you as sender gives your own `actualGasCost` (zero when a paymaster is named), else `receipt.from == you` pays the whole receipt, else nothing. Rule 3 alone fixes Safes; **it costs ZERO extra requests** because `eth_getTransactionReceipt` already returns the logs and `WalletGas` already fetched that receipt. Sponsored gas gets its own running total and the gas ask states both. Logs are matched by keccak-derived TOPIC + sender, never by EntryPoint address, so v0.6/v0.7/v0.8 all match with no list to maintain — but an event is only BELIEVED from a known EntryPoint (a forged one naming you with a paymaster would zero out real gas), and an unknown emitter falls through to the old behaviour and is NAMED by the probe. EntryPoint addresses are doc-derived/UNMEASURED, survivable only because a wrong entry costs a correction we skip rather than a number we assert. `-actingPartiesProbe` is the inventory §292 doesn't cover — who can act as the ACCOUNT: Safe modules (move funds with NO signature, read free off `SafeBridge`'s persisted config snapshots), the EIP-7702 delegate (free, off `WalletSafety`'s existing pass), and ERC-7579 `accountId()`. Both existed only as CHANGE ALERTS, which scroll away. **Ceiling stated in the copy: a 7579 account's modules CANNOT be enumerated** (the standard has `isModuleInstalled` and no listing call; Safe's `getModulesPaginated` is Safe's own), so `modulesUnreadable=YES` means "we can't see", never "nothing installed". Pure logic harnessed in `scripts/wallet-viz-selftest.sh` (222 assertions, 4 mutations). **UNMEASURED against a live smart account** — the bug was found by code reading, not observed. Note Safe's owners/threshold/"2 of 3 signatures collected — your signature is needed"/signer roster/nonce conflicts ALREADY shipped 2026-07-30; don't rebuild them.

- Smart accounts have their own KIND (2026-08-03, prd §294, `AddressBook.Kind.smartAccount` + `WalletActingParties.detectSmartAccount`) — `AddressKind` filed anything with bytecode as `contract`, so watching your own Coinbase Smart Wallet/Biconomy/Kernel account labelled the row **"Contract"** with the square machinery mark and no identicon face. **Identical to the 7702 complaint fixed 2026-07-25** (a delegated EOA read as a contract and lost its face, caught on vitalik.eth — that's why `hasCode` skips `0xef0100`); this is the same ruling for accounts whose bytecode is real. A smart account sits with the WHOS: it keeps the face and its own hue, and says what it is in the label. Detection is asked ONLY of addresses that already have code (an EOA costs what it always did): `accountId()` (ERC-7579 — the account names itself as `vendorname.accountname.semver`, so detection and vendor come in one call) then `entryPoint()` (ERC-4337 — **the answer must be a KNOWN EntryPoint**; any contract can expose a method by that name, and "it answered something" is not evidence). Nil leaves `.contract`, the old behaviour, so a miss is never a wrong claim. **`SmartAccount.vendor` is a SANITIZER, not a formatter** — `accountId()` is attacker-controlled and its first component lands in the app's own chrome, so: letters/digits only, 2–24 chars, else nil (it lives in `WalletUserOps` so the harness compiles it as shipped). `recheckContractsOnce` clears stored `.contract` kinds ONCE so accounts already in the book get re-asked — without it the fix reaches only addresses added from that day on (the `kindRecheck.7702` precedent). Harness caught the TESTS being wrong first: both hostile fixtures were 25 chars, so the length guard rejected them and dropping the character check left the run green — hostile cases must sit inside the length window. **UNMEASURED against a live smart account.**

- Morpho (2026-07-21, prd §157) — the second lending protocol the Wallet seat reads, riding watched wallets like Aave (no account/key/catalog offer). All reads via Morpho's own KEYLESS GraphQL API (`blue-api.morpho.org/graphql`) because Morpho is isolated markets + vaults, not one account read — positions batch across all wallets/chains in one request (`userAddress_in`/`chainId_in`). `Model/MorphoDeFi.swift`: live book (second DeFi tile beside Aave's in the Wallet feed), risk-crossing alert (hf < 1.5, Aave bucket rules), and a settled-activity sweep (Peer cursor shape but TIMESTAMPS not blocks). `-morphoProbe <daysBack|YES>` — NSLogs every watched wallet's book; a numeric spec also rewinds the activity cursors that many DAYS and lands real past events (verified live 2026-07-21 against a real borrower wallet: positions + 12 events + dedupe-to-0 on rerun). The DeFi ask (`WalletDeFiAsk`) now answers across Aave AND Morpho. **Measured quirks, don't "fix" without re-measuring:** `collateralUsd` can be NULL while healthFactor reads (unpriced collateral, still real); dust vault positions are everywhere (burn address holds hundreds) so `holdingFloor` gates rows — except live borrows, never floored; tx amounts are raw BigInts scaled by each asset's `decimals`; market transactions order by `Timestamp` but vault transactions by `Time` (schema asymmetry — either wrong 400s the whole document); `live-integrations.sh` POSTs the app's exact query shapes nightly to catch this drift class.

- Hyperliquid (2026-07-30) — perp positions, spot, and staked HYPE for the Wallet seat, riding watched wallets like Aave/Morpho (no account/key/catalog offer — `Model/HyperliquidDeFi.swift`). All reads via `api.hyperliquid.xyz/info`, keyless. The design problem: a real measured wallet returned 2000 fills spanning 3.5 hours in one read, so `sync()` never lands fills — it diffs the open-position SET against a per-wallet snapshot and lands only "Opened"/"Closed" (with held-duration, grounded in the app's own first-seen timestamp, never a fabricated realized PnL) plus a liquidation-proximity risk crossing (its own 15%-of-mark scale, no HF in this API). `syncUnlocks` lands staked HYPE's unlock as a reconciling `dueAt` row (the ENSExpiry shape). **Measured quirks, don't "fix" without re-measuring:** `clearinghouseState.coin` is a plain symbol, `szi`'s sign is the side; a dust position (0.00001 BTC, $0.65 notional) carried a `liquidationPx` of 8.3 BILLION — every read floors on `positionValue`/spot USD (`WalletIngest.holdingFloor`); spot pricing is NOT `allMids` (returns 0 for every spot token) but `spotMetaAndAssetCtxs`, whose `ctxs` array is NOT positionally aligned with `universe` past the first few entries — match every ctx by its own `coin` field, never by index; `subAccounts` is real (one measured wallet held 5, each its own `clearinghouseState`) and folded into the same book, tagged by name; `portfolio`'s own `accountValueHistory` does NOT reconcile with perps+spot+staked summed independently (~5% measured gap) — never invent a single "Hyperliquid total". **First-sight bug caught live and fixed (2026-07-30):** watching a wallet that already held 22 open positions originally landed 22 "Opened" things in one pass — pure backfill wearing news. `sync()` now seeds the snapshot silently on first sight (the Peer/Morpho cursor-seed rule) while the risk-crossing check still fires on first sight on purpose (the Aave/Morpho precedent — a position already near liquidation when you start watching is exactly what you want to know immediately). `-hyperliquidProbe YES` — NSLogs the book (positions/spot/staked, one line each — a joined single NSLog call truncates past its length limit, the `-todayProbe` lesson, caught live on this exact probe) then runs both syncs. Pairs with `-walletAddress`.

- Aerodrome (2026-07-30) — veAERO locks (Base) for the Wallet seat, riding watched wallets (no account/key/catalog offer — `Model/AerodromeDeFi.swift`). Reads `eth_call` against VotingEscrow/Voter directly (`WalletApprovals.rpcRead(network: "base-mainnet", …)`, the shared host pool) rather than the batch-reading Sugar contracts (`VeSugar.byAccount`) — Sugar's return is a dynamic array of structs that THEMSELVES hold a dynamic array (`votes: DynArray[LpVotes]`), and hand-decoding nested dynamic ABI offsets with no decoding library is a real way to silently ship a wrong number; this file instead walks plain single-return calls (`balanceOf`/`ownerToNFTokenIdList`/`locked`/`balanceOfNFT`/`lastVoted`), each independently verified live. Lands two reconciling-`dueAt` events (the ENSExpiry shape): the WEEKLY VOTE DEADLINE (a lock that hasn't voted this epoch, `lastVoted >= epochStart` read live off the Voter, never computed locally) and LOCK EXPIRY (non-permanent locks only — a permanent lock never expires). `book()` is live state for the "melting lock" read: `balanceOfNFT` (decayed voting power) reads lower than `locked().amount` for a decaying lock and equal for a permanent one — verified against two real locks (2,690 AERO → 2,681 votes; 12,977 AERO → 5,342 votes, ~41% remaining on a 2028 lock). **Measured quirks, don't "fix" without re-measuring:** epoch flips Thu 00:00 UTC, vote window closes Wed 23:00 UTC (read live, not computed — could change); `lastVoted` is a LIFETIME timestamp, not per-epoch — "voted this epoch" is `lastVoted >= epochStart` from the SAME pass's read; `mainnet.base.org` is the ONLY host `WalletApprovals` carries for base-mainnet and 429s under an unpaced burst (a full lock read is 5 sequential calls) — caught live, fixed with a `RequestPacer` actor (200ms between calls) serializing every `eth_call`. **Date-format bug caught live and fixed:** a weekday-only format ("closes Wed 4:00 PM") is ambiguous for a date days out — Pacific-time rendering put both epoch bounds on "Wed" and read as if voteEnd preceded start; titles now carry month+day. NOT built this pass: LP gauge staking positions (`LpSugar.positions`) — same nested-dynamic-struct decode risk as `VeSugar.byAccount`, deferred rather than guessed at. `-aerodromeProbe YES` — NSLogs each lock + the live epoch window, one line each (same truncation lesson as Hyperliquid's probe), then runs the event sync. Pairs with `-walletAddress`.

- `-seedWalletHistory "<usd,usd,…>[|<watched address>]"` — writes a synthetic `WalletStore.ValueSample` line for a watched wallet (the first, or the named one), spaced 4h+ apart so the real `recordSample` throttle can't fold a headless test into one point (prd 126: the Home Wallet row's and Wallet feed's balance sparklines draw off this history, which otherwise only gains a second point after 4 real hours of use). Declared AFTER `-walletAddress` — hooks run in list-declaration order, so it needs a watched wallet to key the history to. The address selector (2026-07-21) is what makes the COMBINED line testable — `combinedValueSamples` only starts once EVERY watched wallet has a sample, so a two-wallet stack needs two seeded lines, one per launch. Each sample carries a synthetic 60/40 ETH/USDC snapshot so the attribution paths (the headline's "Mostly ETH" whisper, the feed's inline mover line, prd §208) have a pair to difference. **Seed at the wallet's REAL scale** — a line seeded orders of magnitude off the live total makes the next real sample land as a vertical spike, and every chart on the screen reads as broken (paid for 2026-07-21).

- `-compositionProbe YES` — the balance card's **"In protocols" strip** (prd §240, 2026-07-31, `Model/WalletComposition.swift`), headless: what's Deposited (Aave/Spark collateral, Morpho vault+supply+collateral, Uniswap principal, Hyperliquid perp+spot), what's Locked, what's Owed, each with the protocols it came from — one NSLog per line (the `-todayProbe` truncation lesson). Pairs with `-walletAddress`. The crown number is UNCHANGED by this feature and stays wallets+exchanges+validators: the ruling was a composition, not a bigger total (a net-worth scalar bakes in accounting choices nobody agrees on, and changing what the total counts would step the sparkline, whose samples were recorded under the old rule). Three invariants not to "simplify": the strip makes NO claim about the crown (on a Zerion outage `WalletIngest` falls back to Alchemy, which returns aTokens as plain tokens, so "not counted above" would be false exactly when the network is worst); LOCKED IS NEVER PRICED (native units — "12,977 AERO"); and a nil `valueUSD` contributes nothing rather than a 0. Hyperliquid and Aerodrome joined `WalletLiveState` this pass — before it they had no seat on any screen. **Deposited and Locked open trays; Owed does NOT** (the Lending card already states health per protocol, so a debt tray would repeat the card beneath it). `WalletLocksTray` draws the MELT — veAERO's decayed voting power against its locked amount (a measured lock: 12,977 AERO → 5,342 votes, ~41% left on a 2028 end), the one visualization here nothing else in the app has; a permanent lock gets a full bar and no date, and staked HYPE gets NO bar because it doesn't decay. Locks are one entry PER LOCK (two veNFTs can end years apart — merging them invents an end date neither has); the per-unit merge exists only in the strip's summary line. Both trays ride the screen's single `.sheet(item:)` and carry the composition VALUE, so a book landing under an open tray can't renumber it. Pure gather is `swift`-harness-tested against the shipped source (40 assertions, incl. the melt's clamp/permanent/no-decay cases) and mutation-tested three ways, not sim-verified.

- `-holdingsWindowProbe YES` / `-holdingsWindow <seconds>` — the holdings freshness window (prd §216, `HoldingsCache` in `Model/WalletIngest.swift`). The probe reads three times and logs each verdict — live → **HIT** (no metered call) → invalidate → live — costing TWO metered `/positions` reads, not three. `-holdingsWindow <s>` overrides the 10-minute default (0 disables it). **The window is on the holdings read ONLY, never on `WalletIngest.refresh`** — holdings report a STATE (a ten-minute-old portfolio value is dated, not wrong), while every other read in that pass reports an EVENT: the Privacy Pools ASP clear-to-withdraw flip, a Peer fill, a new approval, a landed transfer. Those are keyless AND they're the news, so windowing the pass would have made "did my privacy pool clear?" unanswerable for ten minutes at a time to save calls those sweeps never make. Do not move the window up a level. The cache persists across launches (`wallet.holdings.window`), so a force-quit doesn't re-buy holdings read a minute ago; `invalidateHoldingsCache()` (pull-to-refresh) clears the persisted mirror too.
