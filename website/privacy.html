Privacy Policy · Effective November 3, 2026

Your things stay yours.

Casberi is built so your data lives on your device, not on our servers — because we don't run any. This page explains, plainly, what that means.

What Casberi stores, and where

Everything you capture or connect — notes, links, screenshots, events, chats, voice notes, and the things your connected apps bring in — is stored on your device, in Casberi's own local database. Casberi has no backend: your things are never uploaded to us, because there is no "us" server to upload them to.

iCloud sync (optional, off by default)

If you turn on iCloud sync, your things are mirrored through your own private iCloud account using Apple's CloudKit, so they appear on your other devices. Casberi never sees that copy. It is encrypted in transit and on Apple's servers, with Apple holding the keys — unless you turn on Apple's Advanced Data Protection, which makes it end-to-end encrypted, readable only by your own devices. Sync is off until you turn it on, and turning it off (or using Delete things) removes the iCloud copy.

Connecting other apps

When you connect a service — a feed, a social account, a wallet, a calendar — Casberi talks to that service directly from your device to fetch your data. Nothing routes through a Casberi server.

  • A token or key a service needs is stored in your device's Keychain and used only to call that service. A key you paste syncs through iCloud Keychain, end-to-end encrypted, to your other devices; sign-ins and tokens that refresh stay on the device they were made on. Delete access removes them all.
  • For public data (a Bluesky handle, a public feed, a token's price), Casberi reads what's already public — no account of yours is required.
  • Each service you connect has its own privacy policy governing the data on their side; connecting it here doesn't change that.

Wallets, and Tor

On iPhone and iPad, every request that names a wallet — yours or one you follow — goes through Tor, with one route per wallet, so the services that answer it can't learn where you are, or that the wallets you look at are one person's. A few services refuse Tor and are read directly; Casberi says so where it happens. The Mac has no Tor yet, and says so too.

Your account and your money

  • Casberi holds none of your keys. Your account's key is made in your device's Secure Enclave and never leaves it; your recovery key, your private balance's key and your private account's key are kept in your iCloud Keychain. We can't move your money, and have no key to hand anyone who asks.
  • Networks are public. What you send, swap or receive is recorded on that network for anyone to read: the addresses, the amounts and the time.
  • Your private balance uses 0xBow's Privacy Pools on Ethereum. Money going in and coming out is public; what stays hidden is which deposit paid which payment. 0xBow reviews each deposit before it can be spent privately, and its relayer sends what you pay out, so it sees where that money goes and how much. Their privacy policy covers what they keep.
  • Any other token in your private balance uses Railgun on Ethereum. Going in and coming out are public there; what stays hidden is which deposit paid which payment. A payment to someone else is handed to a Railgun broadcaster over Tor, which posts it and takes its fee inside Railgun, so it sees the payment and its fee, never your device’s address or your account.
  • Your private account is on Aztec, where balances, payments and apps are private by default. Your device makes the proof for each transaction itself. Casberi reads Aztec through Tor from a public node (or one you run). Shield, the bridge between Ethereum and Aztec, sees your account's address and the amount when you move money in or out, and checks your Human Passport score first; Human Passport sees your account's address when you open it. An Aztec app you open in Casberi sees your Aztec address only once you connect it. Their privacy policies cover what they keep.
  • Others your moves reach — the services that carry a transaction to the network, the exchanges a swap uses, the services that price a token — see the request itself. Through Tor, they don't see where it came from.

On-device intelligence

On devices that support Apple Intelligence, Casberi uses Apple's on-device model to name your screenshots so you can find them — from your things, on your device. Your data is not sent to Casberi, to Apple's servers, or to any third party to do this. The text inside your screenshots is read the same way, by Apple's Vision framework, on your device. A screenshot that shows a password, a recovery phrase, a key, a card number or a code keeps those lines out of Spotlight and out of anything sent to an agent.

Agents (optional, off by default)

If you connect an agent — Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Venice, Bankr, OpenRouter, Grok, NEAR AI or Muse — what you send it goes from your device directly to that provider, only when you send it, never through a Casberi server, billed to you under that provider's own privacy policy. Its key lives in your Keychain. Your own model is a server you run; what you send goes to the address you gave. Apple Intelligence answers on Apple's Private Cloud Compute, under Apple's privacy policy.

What Casberi does not do

  • No analytics or usage tracking.
  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No selling, sharing, or renting of your data — there's nothing to sell, since we never receive it.
  • No accounts, no sign-up, no password to give us.
  • No push server: notifications are made on your device.

Crash reports

If you choose to share analytics with app developers in your device's settings, Apple may pass Casberi crash reports and performance data, and TestFlight builds may send Apple standard beta diagnostics. That is Apple's collection, under Apple's privacy policy, and you can turn it off in Settings or in the TestFlight app. Performance readings Apple hands the app on your device stay there.

Children

Casberi is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them. (It doesn't knowingly collect information from anyone — see above.)

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with an updated effective date.
