About

What Casberi is, and why

The short version, and the thinking behind it.

The short version

Connect your apps and everything lands in one private feed on your phone — no account, ever. Pin what matters and tag what’s yours, then act on things in the apps they came from. One button asks, organizes, and jumps to your tools. And it’s yours to shape: avatar, background, language, and where your data lives.

The situation

Everything you do creates something — a note, a link, a screenshot, a plan. Each one lands in a different app. The more apps you use, the more places you have to look, and the harder it gets to find and use your own stuff. AI assistants were supposed to help, but each one is another app, with its own history and its own settings. That just adds to the pile.

→

The same things — scattered across ten apps, or together in one feed.

Who it’s for

Casual users — built for you first

Your iPhone is your computer. You use a lot of apps — Notes, Calendar, Reminders, Messages — and maybe one AI app. You don’t want to set anything up. You shouldn’t have to.

Power users — same app, more reach

You run AI agents that work for you — a coding agent on your laptop, jobs on a server. Your phone is how you check on them. You get the same app, with more kinds of things and connections, plus approvals.

What you’ve trying to do

  • Stay mobileYour phone finishes the job.
  • Capture thingsSave any thought, image, or link the moment it happens.
  • Organize thingsKeep order without doing the filing.
  • Find thingsSearch by what something is, not where you left it.
  • Do thingsPlan, book, draft, schedule — all the way to done.
  • Own thingsYour things stay on your phone and leave only when you choose.

How it works today

Right now, your stuff lives in two separate worlds. There’s the Apple world: you screenshot things, jot notes, text yourself, set reminders with Siri — and each app keeps its own pile, with its own search. Then there’s the AI world: chats, threads, and memories inside an assistant app. The two don’t talk. Your assistant can’t put anything on your calendar, and no search covers both.

Your apps
Your AI

Two worlds — and nothing crosses the gap.

If you run agents, it’s four worlds. Your phone, your AI apps, the machines where agents actually work (their output stays there), and the dashboard that supervises them. One job leaves pieces in all four places — the prompt in one, the result in another, the approval in a third. Putting the story back together is your job.

What actually happens

For casual users

  • You save a recipe in Notes with no title — and never find it again. Your travel plan sits 40 messages deep in a chat; at the airport you ask again, and the new answer is missing the hotel address.
  • A dentist reminder gets snoozed six times, because booking takes a call. A dinner plan gets copied by hand into Calendar, Messages, and Safari — and the guest list never leaves the chat.
  • You open your assistant’s settings once, connect nothing, and close it. You try a second assistant, end up with two of everything, and delete it within a week.

For power users

  • A run finishes at 11pm. You find out at 8am, and the follow-up starts a day late. You approve a change bigger than its notification on trust — and it breaks a config.
  • You rebuild in June a skill you wrote in March, because the March version lives in a folder on a machine you retired. A new laptop costs two days of keys and setup.
  • A scheduled job dies quietly. You notice a week later — from the silence.

The problems

The same problems hit everyone — casual users across their apps, power users across their machines and agents:

PROBLEM 1Everything takes copy-pasteTo finish anything, you copy, switch apps, and paste. And if agents do your work, your phone can’t reach them at all.
PROBLEM 2Saving scatters your stuffWhere something ends up depends on how you saved it — and you’ve stuck with that choice.
PROBLEM 3Organizing never endsKeeping things tidy means doing the same filing in every app, forever.
PROBLEM 4Finding means rememberingYou have to recall which app holds the thing — and you find out it’s lost right when you need it.
PROBLEM 5Nothing gets finishedAn assistant’s answer still has to be moved into the real app by hand. That’s where it stalls.
PROBLEM 6Setup doesn’t stickCasual users skip it. Power users redo it for every machine and agent. Either way, none of it carries forward.
PROBLEM 7Every AI app starts from zeroA new assistant brings its own history, memory, and settings. Nothing is shared with the last one.
PROBLEM 8You don’t find outWhat you saved doesn’t speak up when it matters, and your agents finish or fail in silence.

The solution

Casberi is one assistant that works across your apps — not another app that runs separate from them. It gives everything you make one home. Notes, links, screenshots, events, chats, voice notes — they all become things in one feed. Here’s how that fixes each problem:

SOLUTION 1One home for your things.Everything lands in one place. For power users, one job — its prompt, its output, its approval — is one thing, not four scattered pieces.
SOLUTION 2Find by what it is.One search covers everything. You get the thing itself — not the chat it was buried in. Power users can also search by which agent, run, or machine made it.
SOLUTION 3Capture in one gesture.Save from anywhere in one step — no folder to pick, no title to type. Connected apps and machines send their things in on their own.
SOLUTION 4Captures become outcomes.A saved thing carries its next step. An event can go on your calendar, a task can become a reminder — right from the thing.
SOLUTION 5Setup adds up.Casberi is useful before you set up anything, and asks for permissions only when they unlock something. What power users set up once is kept — secrets live only in the Keychain — so a new machine picks up where the old one left off.
SOLUTION 6Not another AI app.Casberi looks and feels like an organizer, not a chatbot. No model picker, no memory panel. Your agents show up as rows in Apps, and their work shows up in your feed.
SOLUTION 7Feels like an Apple product.It follows iOS in type, motion, and touch. It should feel like it came with your phone.
SOLUTION 8Apps are bridges.Connecting an app builds a bridge. Apple apps connect through iOS itself. Services connect with a login or a pasted token. Agents and machines connect with a key. Every app’s page tells you which kind it is.
SOLUTION 9Approvals carry context.When an agent asks to do something, the request shows you what you need to judge it — the exact command, who’s asking, what it touches. Approve or deny with one tap.
SOLUTION 10Status arrives in your feed.Run finished, run failed, scheduled job died — each shows up as a row in your feed. Even something that should have arrived but didn’t.

Your things stay yours

Casberi is a native iPhone app. Your things live in a database on your phone — no Casberi server holds them. iCloud sync is off until you turn it on. Reading and saving work offline. No analytics, no trackers. Answers are written on your own phone.

What’s deliberately not here

Some things are missing on purpose. There’s no separate actions or to-do tab — you act on a thing where the thing is. No automations yet. No model picker and no chatbot persona — Casberi shows results, not a character. No note editor — Casberi collects and connects; your apps stay the editors. No streaks, goals, or guilt mechanics. And no trading, ever.

How to use

The whole app

One screen, a few gestures. Here’s all of it.

Just connect

One screen

Casberi is one screen — no tabs to learn, no account to make. Open it and it’s yours. A row of chips across the top stays put wherever you scroll: Pinned (your board), All (everything you’ve saved), then one chip for each app you’ve connected — the app you just heard from sits up front. Tap a chip to switch.

Down in the corner, a single round button — your Action button — is where everything you do lives. And two small doors up top: your avatar opens Settings, the grid opens your apps.

NextApps →

Apps

Open Apps from the grid icon. Your connected apps sit at the top; below is the catalog — everything you can add, in shelves by category. Each opens a page that says what it does and what shows up in your feed, with one clear button — Connect, Open, Fix, or Soon. This version is built around iOS and the Apple apps you already use.

NextPinned →

Keep tabs

Pinned — your board

Pin anything and it shows up here as a card. Drag cards to arrange them, and tap a pin to grow a card bigger. It’s the one screen that’s just yours — a wallet’s balance, a trip’s plans, your recent chats, laid out the way you like.

NextAll & your apps →

All & your apps

Tap All to see everything, newest first. Tap an app’s chip to see just that app — and the feed takes its shape: Calendar reads like an agenda, Photos becomes a grid, a wallet leads with its balance. Everything you keep tabs on, in one place.

NextOpen a thing →

Open a thing, act on it

Tap any row to open it. From there you do the thing where it belongs — open it in the app it came from, pin it to your board, or share it. Tags group things without folders — one thing can carry many; add or remove them right there.

Some rows let you act in place: start a new email from Gmail, a new event from Calendar, tick a reminder off for real. Casberi keeps tabs; your own apps do the work.

NextAction button →

Take action

Action button

The round button in the corner is your Action button — the one place you do things. Tap it and it opens with “What now?”

  • Ask a question, answered from your own things (“what did I save this week?”). Follow up, and keep an answer as a note.
  • Organize — tell it to tag or rename across everything (“tag these as Trip,” “rename Work to Projects”). It shows you exactly what will change before you tap Apply.
  • Say what you want (“show my work stuff”) and it takes you there.
  • Jump to a tool — create a note, message, email, event, reminder, or web search, or open a ChatGPT or Claude session. The right app opens, ready to go. Nothing is created inside Casberi; you create in the tools you already use.

You can also paste something to save it, or tap the mic and talk.

Make it yours

Settings

Questions

Questions, answered

Do I need an account?

No. There’s nothing to sign up for — Casberi works the moment it’s installed.

Where do my things live?

In a database on your phone. No Casberi server holds them, and iCloud sync stays off until you turn it on. Deleting is two clear choices: Delete things wipes your data (this iPhone and iCloud), Delete access removes every token and key Casberi holds.

What does it cost?

It’s free on TestFlight while it heads to the App Store.

What do I need to run it?

An iPhone. Answers are written on your phone with Apple Intelligence — nothing is sent away, and nothing is billed.

Can it act without asking?

No. Typed text never saves silently, organize commands show you exactly what will change before you tap Apply, and connected wallets are read-only — Casberi can never trade or move funds.
